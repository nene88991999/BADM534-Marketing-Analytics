# Measurement, Disciplinary Authority, and Marketing

An analyst chooses which customer outcomes a decision will recognize. Sales, retention, and estimated willingness to pay enter many models directly. The customer's authority to refuse a practice, the legitimacy of the firm's objective, and the distribution of power require further arguments. The history of economics and marketing helps explain how a particular division of analytical labor became influential. It also identifies alternatives that remained available within these disciplines.

## Cowles and the Role of Theory

The Cowles Commission adopted “Science is Measurement” at its founding in 1932. In 1952, it changed the motto to “Theory and Measurement.” The contemporary institutional history explains that measurement did not exhaust the scientific work the commission had come to undertake (Christ, 1952, pp. 61–62). Its later institutional account records the same change ([Cowles Foundation, n.d.](https://cowles.yale.edu/about-cowles)). The episode supports an argument about the authority of formal theory and quantitative evidence. Treating it as an unqualified commitment to whatever is immediately observable would misstate the history.

The critique of business-cycle measurement concerned the economic interpretation of observed regularities. Description required a structure connecting observations to economic relations (Koopmans, 1947). The methodological history also records an expanding role for pure theory alongside econometrics (Malinvaud, 1983). These developments challenged measurement without interpretation. Their contribution to causal analysis remains useful when examining whether electricity prices represent household burdens or whether observed purchases reveal consent.

The philosophical question concerns the boundary of that contribution. A theory can organize evidence while taking the firm's objective as given. Identification can establish a parameter within a model while leaving the justification of the modeled objective unresolved. These are contemporary applications of the historical record. Neither the motto nor the development of econometrics establishes that the institution deliberately rejected Kantian philosophy.

## Three Changes in Disciplinary Authority

| Historical Development | Evidence | Consequence for Marketing Dialectics |
|---|---|---|
| Separation of economic analysis from judgment about ends | Economics was defined as neutral among ends, with a distinction between factual propositions and ethical valuation (Robbins, 1935, pp. 24, 148–151). | An efficient means does not establish a justified end. The decision maker must supply and defend the end. |
| Quantitative science as an institutional priority | Foundation funding helped reshape business schools around academic disciplines, doctoral training, and scientific research (Khurana et al., 2011). | Research priorities can emerge from funding, training, and professional incentives. Measurement choices have an institutional history. |
| Marketing organized around managerial decisions | Historical accounts describe the managerial and scientific turn and the later fragmentation of scholarship (Wilkie and Moore, 2003, 2012). | A firm's decision problem can become the discipline's default frame, reducing the visibility of costs borne outside the firm. |

The separation of economics from virtue was historically contested. The same account that separates factual and ethical propositions encourages economists to think widely about ethical questions (Robbins, 1935, p. 150). A historical and conceptual account connects the technocratic ideal to assumptions about agreement on social purposes and the division of intellectual labor between economics and ethics (Schliesser, 2016). This supplies a more direct foundation for the philosophical critique than the original Cowles motto. It concerns whose reasons remain within the economist's professional task.

Marketing provides particularly useful evidence because the firm's standpoint became central to its teaching and research. The societal perspective lost prominence in parts of the mainstream while developing in specialized outlets. The change was reversible in some institutional settings: the 2007 marketing definition explicitly included society at large (Wilkie and Moore, 2012, pp. 61–65). The history supports an account of competing orientations and unequal disciplinary attention. The article's evidence does not isolate whether publication patterns arose from submissions, editorial decisions, or both.

## Quantification and Public Justification

Quantification can make decisions portable across organizations and credible to people who lack personal access to the decision maker. Its authority therefore has social and institutional foundations (Porter, 1995). An analysis of twentieth-century United States policymaking traces how efficiency, incentives, and competition became influential criteria across policy institutions, displacing some arguments framed around equality, rights, and constraints on corporate power (Berman, 2022, chapter 1).

For a pricing decision, this history motivates a concrete inquiry. Who selected profit or aggregate surplus as the objective? Which burdens disappear when the analysis moves to an average? Which claims require a measured effect before they receive attention? The historical account makes these choices available for criticism. A useful statistical estimate can coexist with an objection to the institution's chosen criterion.

## Phenomena and Noumena

The distinction between phenomena and noumena concerns the limits of theoretical knowledge and the standpoint from which objects are considered. A latent preference, an unobserved cost, and a missing household bill remain possible objects of empirical inquiry. Their present absence from a dataset does not make them noumena. The relevant Kantian application concerns the authority assigned to empirical knowledge when judging persons and permissible conduct. The existing [discussion of phenomena and noumena](../../06_Lecture_Notes/modules/M09_Marketing_Dialectics.md) develops that distinction in §§9.41–9.43.

A defensible historical thesis is that influential institutions increasingly privileged questions answerable through formal models, measurement, and managerial choice. The stronger claim that economics and marketing uniformly abandoned noumenal questions requires evidence these histories do not supply. A history of consumer theory also challenges the familiar account in which psychology simply disappeared and later returned (Hands, 2010). An early behaviorist manifesto provides a clearer example of explicitly prioritizing prediction and control while setting introspection aside (Watson, 1913). Consciousness and Kantian noumena still refer to different philosophical problems.

## Evidence and Decisions

Identification, estimation, and inference acquired a central place in econometrics while statistical decision theory received less attention. A decision-theoretic account evaluates an analytical rule through its consequences across plausible states of the world, including states outside a convenient model (Manski, 2021, pp. 2828–2833). This provides a connection from disciplinary history to [evidentiary standards and asymmetric error costs](../47_Evidence_Standards_and_Error_Costs/README.md).

The resulting criticism concerns a transfer of authority. A standard designed to discipline a causal claim becomes a standard deciding whether a consumer deserves protection. That transfer requires a justification addressing the consequences of action and inaction. Statistical accuracy contributes to that justification; it does not settle the distribution of those consequences.
