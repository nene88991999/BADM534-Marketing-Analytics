## Cowles and Econometrics

### H01: About Us

Cowles Foundation (n.d.). Institutional history, founding dates, and motto.

[Source](https://cowles.yale.edu/about-cowles). The 1952 change from Science is Measurement to Theory and Measurement complicates a simple account of empiricism. The institution gives formal theory an explicit role.

**Inspected:** Institutional webpage inspected. **Copy:** linked.

### H02: History of the Cowles Commission for Research in Economics

Carl F. Christ (1952). Twenty-year report; pp. 61–62.

[Source](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/history-cowles.pdf). Contemporary explanation of the motto change supports attention to the expanding scope of theoretical work. Use the primary history alongside later accounts.

**Inspected:** Scanned pages 61–64 visually inspected; full report downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H03: The Cowles Commission’s Contributions to Econometrics at Chicago, 1939–1955

Carl F. Christ (1994). Journal of Economic Literature 32:30–59.

[Source](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-08/misc-christ-1994.pdf). A retrospective history of the Chicago period supplies further context for identification and econometric methodology. It is an additional reading, not the sole basis for the guide’s historical claims.

**Inspected:** Title and opening page inspected; indexed excerpts consulted. Scanned full article downloaded, not fully read. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H04: Econometric Methodology at the Cowles Commission: Rise and Maturity

Edmond Malinvaud (1983). Fiftieth-anniversary lecture, June 4, 1983; opening discussion.

[Source](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-12/50th-malinvaud.pdf). The history describes measurement, econometrics, and pure theory together. Formalization did not require restricting the discipline to immediately observable objects.

**Inspected:** Opening pages inspected; complete lecture downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H05: Measurement Without Theory

Tjalling C. Koopmans (1947). Review of Economics and Statistics 29(3):161–172.

[Source](https://fairmodel.econ.yale.edu/ec439/koop1.pdf). Observed regularities require an economic structure for interpretation. This methodological argument leaves separate questions about the justification of economic objectives.

**Inspected:** Opening critique and theoretical interpretation inspected; full article downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

## Economics and Ethics

### H06: An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science

Lionel Robbins (1935). Second edition; pp. 24 and 148–151.

[Source](https://milescorak.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/02/robbins-essay-nature-significance-economic-science.pdf). The definition of economics as neutral among ends is direct evidence of a boundary between economic analysis and ethical judgment. It does not imply that economists never make moral arguments.

**Inspected:** Selected passages on ends and ethical valuation inspected; complete volume downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H07: The Separation of Economics from Virtue: A Historical–Conceptual Introduction

Eric Schliesser (2016). Economics and the Virtues, pp. 141–164; saved 2015 proof.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780198701392.003.0008). The technocratic ideal relies on assumptions about agreement on ends and disciplinary expertise. Mathematical formalization and the separation from ethics are related historical questions with distinct mechanisms.

**Inspected:** Abstract and opening historical-conceptual argument inspected; complete chapter proof downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H08: From Interwar Pluralism to Postwar Neoclassicism

Mary S. Morgan; Malcolm Rutherford, editors (1998). History of Political Economy 30, annual supplement.

[Source](https://www.bibliovault.org/BV.book.epl?ISBN=9780822323358). The collection provides historical routes into changes in pluralism, patronage, and formalization. Individual chapters require further reading before assigning their specific arguments.

**Inspected:** Publisher description and contents only; no full text downloaded. **Copy:** linked.

### H17: Critique of Pure Reason

Immanuel Kant (1781/1787). Meiklejohn translation, 1855; phenomena/noumena discussion.

[Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/4280/4280-h/4280-h.htm). Noumena concern limits of theoretical knowledge. Latent tastes and missing bills remain possible empirical objects; the philosophical distinction should not be reduced to missing data.

**Inspected:** Existing course treatment and local public-domain text; no new download. **Copy:** existing local copy.

### H18: Economics, psychology and the history of consumer choice theory

D. Wade Hands (2010). Cambridge Journal of Economics 34(4):633–648.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/cje/bep045). The history challenges the simple story of psychology first being removed from choice theory and later restored. It provides a check against a uniform historical narrative.

**Inspected:** Publisher abstract inspected. Attempted preprint retrieval did not return a PDF. **Copy:** unavailable.

## Marketing and Management

### H09: Scholarly Research in Marketing: Exploring the “4 Eras” of Thought Development

William L. Wilkie; Elizabeth S. Moore (2003). Journal of Public Policy & Marketing 22(2):116–146.

[Source](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jppm.22.2.116.17639). The historical periodization identifies a managerial and scientific era followed by fragmentation. Pair with the accessible 2012 article for the societal consequences and qualifications.

**Inspected:** Publisher abstract and bibliographic information inspected; no full text downloaded. **Copy:** linked.

### H10: Expanding our understanding of marketing in society

William L. Wilkie; Elizabeth S. Moore (2012). Journal of the Academy of Marketing Science 40:53–73; online 2011.

[Source](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-011-0277-y). Societal inquiry lost attention in parts of mainstream marketing while developing in specialized outlets. The 2007 definition restored society explicitly. The evidence does not isolate editorial decisions from submission patterns.

**Inspected:** Sections on marginalization, journal evidence, and definitions, pp. 61–65, inspected. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H11: From Higher Aims to Hired Hands

Rakesh Khurana (2007). Princeton University Press.

[Source](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691145877/from-higher-aims-to-hired-hands). The history of business schools connects professional ideals to changing institutional purposes. Use as a broader reading rather than as evidence that every business school followed the same path.

**Inspected:** Publisher description only; complete book not obtained. **Copy:** linked.

### H12: How Foundations Think: The Ford Foundation as a Dominating Institution in the Field of American Business Schools

Rakesh Khurana; Kenneth Kimura; Marion Fourcade (2011). Harvard Business School Working Paper 11-070.

[Source](https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/11-070.pdf). Foundation resources and organizational priorities helped reshape business education toward disciplinary science. This is a mechanism for the selection of research priorities, not evidence of a universal rejection of ethics.

**Inspected:** Abstract, introduction, and selected funding passages inspected; complete working paper downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

## Quantification and Public Reason

### H13: Trust in Numbers: The Pursuit of Objectivity in Science and Public Life

Theodore M. Porter (1995). Princeton University Press; linked reissue.

[Source](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691208411/trust-in-numbers). Quantification has an institutional role in producing authority and trust. Its social function can be examined alongside the technical quality of a particular measurement.

**Inspected:** Publisher account only; full book not obtained. **Copy:** linked.

### H14: Thinking like an Economist: How Efficiency Replaced Equality in U.S. Public Policy

Elizabeth Popp Berman (2022). Author-posted uncorrected chapter 1 proof.

[Source](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691167381/thinking-like-an-economist). The argument concerns the rise of an economic style in United States policy institutions. It supplies a documented setting where efficiency displaced some other public reasons, rather than a claim about all economics.

**Inspected:** Opening chapter, especially pp. 1–4, inspected; chapter only downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### H15: Psychology as the Behaviorist Views It

John B. Watson (1913). Psychological Review 20:158–177.

[Source](https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Watson/views.htm). Prediction and control of behavior receive explicit priority while introspection is displaced. Consciousness is not synonymous with Kantian noumena, and the manifesto does not describe all later psychology.

**Inspected:** Opening manifesto inspected in HTML. **Copy:** linked.

### H16: From quantification to methodolatry

Kurt Danziger (1990). Constructing the Subject, chapter 9, pp. 136–155.

[Source](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/constructing-the-subject/from-quantification-to-methodolatry/D134BABC20140B0B651832DF6619BBC3). The historical inquiry asks how psychological attributes became quantitative objects. It motivates examining the practices that produce a measurement rather than treating numerical form as self-explanatory.
