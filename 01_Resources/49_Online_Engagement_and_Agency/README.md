# Marketing Research, Online Engagement, and Agency

A platform can retain a person by serving a purpose the person endorses or by making departure harder. Marketing publications help explain how sequences, rewards, and records sustain activity. The critical question is whether the commercial use respects the person's purposes and authority to stop.

This collection contains ten marketing and consumer-research papers, one economics paper, three company documents, and one regulatory notice. It is a focused selection of mechanisms and documented applications, with no claim to estimate their prevalence across the literature. The [catalog](catalog.json) records author metadata, inspected passages, access limits, and local retrieval hashes. IDs E01–E15 refer to this collection only.

## Evidence and Interpretation

| Claim | Evidence Required | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A feature can change repeated behavior | Experiment or an adequately supported empirical design | E01–E05 |
| A related feature was deployed | Product evidence identifying the feature and date | E08, E13, E14 |
| A company uses the research in its rationale | Explicit citation or other documented connection | E12 cites E02 |
| A feature caused unwanted continuation or harm | Relevant user outcomes, comparison, and population | Requires more than a retention effect |
| A firm ought to change the design | Supported factual premises, a defended principle, and feasible alternatives | M9 §§9.74–9.75 |

A corporate citation leaves the consequence question open. A deployed feature does not establish that an academic paper originated it. High use alone distinguishes neither enjoyment from compulsion nor an economic habit model from a clinical diagnosis. The notes preserve these distinctions while allowing proportionate protection before every uncertainty is resolved.

## Philosophical Assessment

Repeated activity can become an ontologically inadequate substitute for the person whose interests supposedly justify the design. The criticism must identify the omitted relation: a stopping instruction, a learning purpose, a spending boundary, or a burden on someone outside the retained sample. An analyst can estimate the chosen outcome precisely while leaving that omission intact.

Kantian respect concerns the person's authority to set ends. Contractualist reasoning compares objections to principles governing the design, including a feasible alternative and the interests of users who want extended access. Value capture concerns the possibility that a simplified goal displaces deliberation about what matters. These are applications of the philosophical arguments in M9 §§9.41, 9.47, and 9.67; the empirical papers do not themselves prove the normative conclusions.
