# Statistical Virtues and Ontological Adequacy

A statistical procedure can answer its chosen question well while leaving the object of a broader claim inadequately represented. The criticism of ontological laziness identifies the substitution, the omitted relation, and its consequence for the decision. It applies to arguments and uses of results; it supplies no diagnosis of a researcher’s motives.

The teaching material is in [M1 §1.24, M6 §6.46.6, and M9 §§9.66–9.69](../../06_Lecture_Notes/README.md), Sessions 1 and 14, and the [optional argument exercise](../../07_Labs/Philosophical_Arguments/README.md#statistical-virtues-and-the-missing-object). The existing [hierarchical-method example](../../03_Data_and_Code/Heterogeneity_and_Consumer_Protection/METHOD_CROSSWALK.md) supplies the exact statistical connection and its synthetic scope.

## Reading Sequence

Begin with V01 on abstraction, V02 on ontology and knowledge, V04 on records and explanatory objects, V10 on metric-driven changes in values, and V11 on justification to affected people. Use V06 and V09 to examine criticism and error consequences. V14 supplies a serious empiricist reply. V07–V08 supply institutional history; their annotations are based on publisher accounts. These readings disagree about realism, knowledge, and justification. Each argument supports a specified premise; the collection preserves these disagreements.

## Annotated Sources

### V01: Science and the Modern World

(Alfred North Whitehead, 1925). [Source](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/68611).

**Focus:** Chapters III–IV; misplaced concreteness and abstraction.

**Application:** Distinguish a useful representation from an exhaustive account of a person or burden. The criticism requires identifying the consequential abstraction.

**Inspection:** Indexed primary-text passages around printed pp. 51 and 59 inspected. Gutenberg returned HTTP 503 and an alternate download did not yield a PDF; full text was not retrieved.

### V02: Theorising Ontology

(Roy Bhaskar, 2007). [Source](https://internationalcentreforcriticalrealism.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2013/04/07-1-bhaskar-theorising-ontology-2007.pdf).

**Focus:** Contributions to Social Ontology, printed pp. 192–194.

**Application:** Distinguish what exists from a method of knowing it. Missing estimates leave claims unsettled; they do not define the limits of reality.

**Inspection:** Downloaded chapter excerpt; pp. 192–194 inspected, including the defense of specific, separable scientific knowledge.

### V03: Empiricism and the Philosophy of Mind

(Wilfrid Sellars, 1956). [Source](https://www.ditext.com/sellars/epm8.html).

**Focus:** Part VIII, §§32–38.

**Application:** Identify the concepts and justificatory relations supporting the interpretation of an observation. A record does not authorize every label attached to it.

**Inspection:** Primary HTML Part VIII inspected; no claim to have reviewed the entire work.

### V04: Saving the Phenomena

(James Bogen; James Woodward, 1988). [Source](https://doi.org/10.2307/2185445).

**Focus:** The Philosophical Review 97(3):303–352; pp. 305–306.

**Application:** Keep particular records, inferred processes, and their explanatory roles distinct. Their scientific use of phenomena differs from Kantian phenomena/noumena.

**Inspection:** Full article downloaded; opening argument and data/phenomena distinction on pp. 305–306 inspected.

### V05: The Dappled World: A Study of the Boundaries of Science

(Nancy Cartwright, 1999). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9781139167093.001).

**Focus:** Introduction, pp. 1–20; local laws and their scope.

**Application:** Ask what institutional conditions sustain a model’s success before extending its interpretation. A good local result supplies no universal claim.

**Inspection:** Publisher introduction summary and contents inspected. Full chapter not obtained; the scope argument is limited to those passages.

### V06: How Values Can Be Good for Science

(Helen E. Longino, 2004). [Source](https://www.ugr.es/~zink/mono/Longino2004.pdf).

**Focus:** Science, Values, and Objectivity, pp. 127–142; especially pp. 134–135.

**Application:** Allow effective criticism of the target and background assumptions, including criticism from people outside the technical community.

**Inspection:** Existing local chapter read at pp. 134–135: venues, uptake, public standards, tempered equality, and provisional background assumptions.

### V07: Trust in Numbers: The Pursuit of Objectivity in Science and Public Life

(Theodore M. Porter, 1995). [Source](https://press.princeton.edu/books/ebook/9780691210544/trust-in-numbers).

**Focus:** Institutional authority and the social uses of quantification.

**Application:** Examine why comparability and impersonal procedures receive authority. Social origins do not by themselves invalidate a measure.

**Inspection:** Official publisher account and original copyright year inspected; full book not obtained.

### V08: Objectivity

(Lorraine Daston; Peter Galison, 2007). [Source](https://www.zonebooks.org/books/5-objectivity).

**Focus:** Truth-to-nature, mechanical objectivity, and trained judgment.

**Application:** Distinguish epistemic ideals and the practices that sustain them. Their historical variation motivates scrutiny of a virtue’s role, not its blanket rejection.

**Inspection:** Official publisher account and bibliographic details inspected; full book not obtained.

### V09: Inductive Risk and Values in Science

(Heather Douglas, 2000). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1086/392855).

**Focus:** Philosophy of Science 67(4):559–579; consequences of error.

**Application:** Assess how mistaken reassurance and mistaken accusation affect people. Values can bear on evidentiary judgment without replacing evidence.

**Inspection:** Existing full-text copy and course source assessment consulted; opening inductive-risk argument previously inspected.

### V10: Value Capture

(C. Thi Nguyen, 2024). [Source](https://doi.org/10.26556/jesp.v27i3.3048).

**Focus:** Journal of Ethics and Social Philosophy 27(3):469–504; simplified values and deliberation.

**Application:** Examine when an engagement or accuracy score becomes the goal itself and displaces deliberation about customer purposes.

**Inspection:** Published abstract, metadata, and license inspected; 52-page author preprint downloaded and opening pp. 1–3 inspected. Preprint pagination is not published pagination.

### V11: What We Owe to Each Other

(T. M. Scanlon, 1998). [Source](https://www.hup.harvard.edu/books/9780674004238).

**Focus:** Chapter 5; principles and reasons for reasonable rejection.

**Application:** Require a separate argument about the burdens imposed by a decision. Statistical quality is one consideration among reasons owed to affected people.

**Inspection:** Existing course excerpt, pp. 192–196, and source assessment consulted; this course application does not assign a numerical proof threshold.

### V12: Critique of Pure Reason

(Immanuel Kant, 1781/1787). [Source](../15_Marketing_Dialectics/01_Kant_Primary_Texts/public_domain_texts/Kant_CritiqueOfPureReason_Meiklejohn1855.html).

**Focus:** B306–B311; the negative concept of noumenon.

**Application:** Distinguish a limit of theoretical cognition from an ordinary unmeasured variable. Missing billing data and latent preferences remain empirical questions.

**Inspection:** Existing public-domain translation and course §9.41 source assessment consulted. The negative limiting concept does not establish a particular hidden harm.

### V13: Public Artifacts, Intentions and Norms

(Amie L. Thomasson, 2014). [Source](https://www.amiethomasson.org/_files/ugd/63fc8a_840cc91860474dfa99802928543989fb.doc).

**Focus:** Artefact Kinds, pp. 45–62; §§5.2–5.4.

**Application:** Examine a dataset’s purposes, recognized uses, and governance while preserving the distinction between records and the events they represent.

**Inspection:** Existing author manuscript and artifact source assessment consulted; dataset and NDA applications are course extensions.

### V14: The Scientific Image

(Bas C. van Fraassen, 1980). [Source](https://doi.org/10.1093/0198244274.001.0001).

**Focus:** Printed pp. 18–19; observability, existence, and empirical adequacy.

**Application:** Test a serious empiricist reply. A limit on belief or observability is not a denial that unobserved things exist.

**Inspection:** Downloaded primary excerpts; printed pp. 18–19 read. Publisher abstract independently confirms the constructive-empiricist position.

### V15: The Fate of Knowledge

(Helen E. Longino, 2002). [Source](https://press.princeton.edu/books/paperback/9780691088761/the-fate-of-knowledge).

**Focus:** Chapter 1, Introduction: The Rational-Social Dichotomy.

**Application:** Examine how social criticism and cognitive justification can support one another. Avoid assuming social provenance alone defeats knowledge.

**Inspection:** Official ten-page chapter excerpt downloaded and opening pp. 1–5 inspected. Publisher lists copyright 2002 and release November 2001; citation follows copyright year.

### V16: The Crisis of European Sciences and Transcendental Phenomenology

(Edmund Husserl, 1970). [Source](https://www.marxists.org/reference/subject/philosophy/works/ge/husserl.htm).

**Focus:** English translation, §22; intentionality and psychic atomism.

**Application:** Ask whether a representation of isolated responses loses the directedness and meaning of an action. Keep phenomenological and realist arguments distinct.

**Inspection:** Primary HTML §22 inspected. This excerpt does not include §9 on mathematization; no claim to have inspected that section.
