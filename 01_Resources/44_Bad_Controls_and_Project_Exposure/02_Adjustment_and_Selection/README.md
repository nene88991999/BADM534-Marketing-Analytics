## BC03: The Consequences of Adjustment for a Concomitant Variable that Has Been Affected by the Treatment

Paul R. Rosenbaum. JRSS A, 1984, 147(5), 656–666.

[Original source](https://doi.org/10.2307/2981697) | [Public PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/peterg/Vaccine2006/articles/Rosenbaum1984.pdf)

**Read:** Summary and introduction, printed p. 656.

Treatment-affected covariates can bias adjustment even with randomized assignment. Separate the treatment-assignment problem from the distortion introduced by conditioning after assignment.

**Access and scope:** Scanned PDF; opening substantive page visually inspected. No machine-readable full-text conversion supplied.

## BC04: Endogenous Selection Bias: The Problem of Conditioning on a Collider Variable

Felix Elwert; Christopher Winship. Annual Review of Sociology, 2014, 40, 31–53.

[Original source](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-071913-043455) | [Public PDF](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~elwert/causality/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/1-Elwert-Winship-2014.pdf)

**Read:** Introduction, graphical explanations, selection examples.

Selection can occur through regression adjustment, sample restrictions, or missing observations. Conditioning on a common consequence can associate its causes. Completion and inclusion deserve a causal argument as much as a named regression control.

**Access and scope:** Author manuscript; introduction and collider discussion inspected.

## BC05: How Conditioning on Posttreatment Variables Can Ruin Your Experiment and What to Do about It

Jacob M. Montgomery; Brendan Nyhan; Michelle Torres. AJPS, 2018, 62(3), 760–775; author manuscript.

[Original source](https://doi.org/10.1111/ajps.12357) | [Public PDF](https://cpb-us-e1.wpmucdn.com/sites.dartmouth.edu/dist/5/2293/files/2021/03/post-treatment-bias.pdf)

**Read:** Introduction, conditioning, subsetting, recommendations.

Randomization protects the assigned comparison. Dropping participants after assignment or restricting to responders can undo that protection. Marketing applications include completed purchases, retained subscribers, and experiment participants who opened a message.

**Access and scope:** Author manuscript; abstract and post-assignment selection discussion inspected.

## BC06: Overadjustment Bias and Unnecessary Adjustment in Epidemiologic Studies

Enrique F. Schisterman; Stephen R. Cole; Robert W. Platt. Epidemiology, 2009, 20(4), 488–495.

[Original source](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2744485/)

**Read:** Overadjustment versus unnecessary adjustment.

Removing a mediator from a total-effect pathway and adding a redundant predictor are different decisions. Bias and loss of precision require separate diagnoses. A changed coefficient alone establishes neither.

**Access and scope:** Publisher/repository abstract and indexed text inspected; PDF not downloaded.
