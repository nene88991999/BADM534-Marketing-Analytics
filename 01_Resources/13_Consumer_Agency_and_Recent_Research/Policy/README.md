# Subscription Cancellation and Consumer Choice

A cancellation process determines whether a customer's decision to leave becomes an effective end to recurring charges. Retention can rise because service quality improves, because a consumer knowingly accepts a useful offer, or because the interface obstructs exit. Those mechanisms have different implications for customer welfare and for the interpretation of lifetime value.

Status checked November 5, 2026. The entries below distinguish legal instruments, jurisdictions, covered transactions, and effective dates. They are a teaching source record, not a complete statement of every applicable subscription law.

## United States Federal Rules

The Eighth Circuit vacated the FTC's 2024 Negative Option Rule on July 8, 2025 after finding a procedural failure involving the required preliminary regulatory analysis. The decision removed that rule; it did not repeal separate statutory protections. See the [court's opinion](https://ecf.ca8.uscourts.gov/opndir/25/07/243137P.pdf), especially pages 19-23, and the local [PDF](Eighth_Circuit_2025_Vacatur.pdf).

Effective February 12, 2026, the FTC recodified the earlier prenotification rule to conform to the court's decision. That older rule has a narrower scope centered on prenotification plans for merchandise. The FTC then issued an advance notice of proposed rulemaking on March 13, 2026 seeking input on negative-option practices. That notice does not itself establish a replacement cancellation obligation. The October 14, 2026 information-collection notice also concerns the restored earlier rule. Sources: [February final action](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-02-12/pdf/2026-02866.pdf), [March notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-03-13/pdf/2026-04952.pdf), and [October notice](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2026-10-14/pdf/2026-20971.pdf). Local copies: [February](FTC_2026_Recodification.pdf), [March](FTC_2026_Proposed_Rulemaking.pdf), and [October](FTC_2026_Information_Collection.pdf).

The Restore Online Shoppers' Confidence Act, enacted December 29, 2010, separately addresses online negative-option transactions. Section 4 requires clear disclosure of material terms before obtaining billing information, express informed consent before charging, and a simple mechanism to stop recurring charges. These requirements provide a federal baseline for the covered transactions. Source: [Public Law 111-345, Section 4](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-111publ345/pdf/PLAW-111publ345.pdf), codified at 15 U.S.C. §8403; [local PDF](ROSCA_2010.pdf).

## California

California strengthened its Automatic Renewal Law effective July 1, 2025. The Attorney General's September 2025 guidance describes coverage of consumer automatic-renewal and continuous-service arrangements, including free trials that become paid subscriptions. It emphasizes express consent, reminders, and straightforward cancellation. Consumers who enroll online must be able to cancel online without obstructive or delaying steps. Source: [California Attorney General's guidance](https://oag.ca.gov/news/press-releases/attorney-general-bonta-issues-consumer-alert-california%E2%80%99s-automatic-renewal-law).

## New York City

New York City's rule took effect October 1, 2026. It addresses automatic-renewal and continuous-service arrangements through disclosure, consent, and cancellation requirements. Section 5-110.2 requires a simple cancellation process that is as easy as the method used to enroll, with cancellation available through enrollment media. It bars obstruction and unreasonable delay. Retention offers may be made subject to the rule's restrictions; they may not obstruct or unreasonably delay cancellation. Section 5-110.3 contains sector exclusions, including specified regulated financial, insurance, alarm, and franchise-related arrangements. Coverage must be assessed before applying the rule to a business. Sources: [official city guidance](https://www.nyc.gov/main/click-to-cancel) and [adopted rule](https://www.nyc.gov/content/dam/nycgov/nyc-main/pdf/2026/Click-to-Cancel-Rule.pdf). The official text was read online; the direct PDF transfer returned an access error.

## Measurement and Evaluation

For a cancellation flow, define the eligible population as customers who initiate a cancellation attempt. Measure successful cancellation, elapsed time, required interactions, subsequent charges, refunds, understanding of any retention offer, and later complaints. Preserve enrollment channel, jurisdiction, and contract dates. A lower cancellation rate can reflect useful voluntary retention or a failure to execute the customer's intent; the event log alone may not separate them.

An evaluation should compare lawful, transparent service or cancellation designs and track whether customers achieve their stated goals. A jurisdiction-level policy comparison also needs a treatment date, covered population, comparable controls, pre-policy trends, and attention to firm-wide interface changes that spill across jurisdictions. A rule's effective date is an intervention date, not automatic causal identification.
