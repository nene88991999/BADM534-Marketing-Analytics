# Favorite Possessions Protect Subjective Well-Being Under Income Inequality

Jingshi (Joyce) Liu; Amy N. Dalton; Anirban Mukhopadhyay. Journal of Marketing Research. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/00222437221141053).

Online publication: 2023-03-22. Issue publication: 2024-08.

## Managerial Implications

Attention to favorite possessions can buffer subjective well-being when income inequality is salient by reducing wealth comparisons. Consumer support can emphasize meaningful possessions already owned. The evidence concerns psychological responses, rather than the elimination of material inequality.

## Consumer Agency

Reducing harmful comparison may support agency. Emotional reassurance should accompany, rather than obscure, consequential material information.

Assessment: protective. Mechanism features: emotion_identity, social_cues. A potential misuse is an analytical inference, not a claim about the authors' intentions or proof of harm.
