# Annotated Readings

Catalog IDs refer to this folder’s `catalog.json`. Inspection scope distinguishes source passages from abstracts and publisher previews. Source copies with no established redistribution permission remain in the local `readings/` directory.

## Demand and Identification

### E01: Bayesian Analysis of Random Coefficient Logit Models Using Aggregate Data

Renna Jiang; Puneet Manchanda; Peter E. Rossi (2007). August 2007 working paper; published Journal of Econometrics 149(2):136–148, 2009.

[Source](https://ice.uchicago.edu/2008_presentations/Rossi/bayesian_agg_share_092607.pdf). The aggregate-share likelihood imposes distributions of heterogeneity and common demand shocks. Posterior precision and simulated robustness must be interpreted relative to those restrictions.

**Inspected:** Abstract, model, and likelihood discussion inspected through web PDF; complete 2007 working paper downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E02: Even the Rich Can Make Themselves Poor: A Critical Examination of IV Methods in Marketing Applications

Peter E. Rossi (2014). Marketing Science 33(5):655–672.

[Source](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2343916). Weak or invalid instruments can perform poorly. The abstract supports scrutiny of instrument quality; it supplies no evidence that the author intends to shield firms from enforcement.

**Inspected:** Indexed abstract and bibliographic record only; no full text obtained. **Copy:** linked.

### E03: Plausibly Exogenous

Timothy G. Conley; Christian B. Hansen; Peter E. Rossi (2012). Review of Economics and Statistics 94(1):260–272.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.1162/REST_a_00139). Sensitivity analysis relaxes the exclusion restriction and can remain informative. This is counterevidence to a blanket attribution of demands for perfect identification to this methodological tradition.

**Inspected:** Abstract and author publication listing inspected; full text not obtained. **Copy:** linked.

### E04: Identification in Differentiated Products Markets Using Market Level Data

Steven T. Berry; Philip A. Haile (2010). Cowles Discussion Paper 1744, February 2010; published Econometrica 82(5):1749–1797, 2014.

[Source](https://cowles.yale.edu/sites/default/files/2022-09/d1744.pdf). Rich heterogeneity is compatible with restrictions that identify demand, costs, and discriminate among conduct models. Identification can support enforcement rather than necessarily preventing it.

**Inspected:** Abstract and opening identification argument inspected. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E05: Bayesian and Frequentist Inference in Partially Identified Models

Hyungsik Roger Moon; Frank Schorfheide (2012). Econometrica 80(2):755–782; local copy is NBER Working Paper 14882, April 2009.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA8360). Bayesian credible sets can lie inside the identified set asymptotically. Reporting the identified set and conditional prior makes the source of precision more transparent.

**Inspected:** Publisher abstract and opening two pages of the 2009 working paper inspected. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E16: rbayesBLP: Bayesian Analysis of Random Coefficient Logit Models Using Aggregate Data

Peter Rossi; K. Kim (n.d.). bayesm reference documentation, checked November 27, 2026.

[Source](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/bayesm/html/rbayesBLP.html). The implementation distinguishes numerical integration over preferences, priors, and a model of endogenous prices. Integration is not an assumption of competitive pricing or a finding of legal permissibility.

**Inspected:** Model, priors, instrumental-variable extension, and algorithm documentation inspected; no package executed. **Copy:** linked.

## Decision Theory and Law

### E06: Econometrics for Decision Making: Building Foundations Sketched by Haavelmo and Wald

Charles F. Manski (2021). Econometrica 89(6):2827–2853.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA17985). Decision rules can be assessed by their consequences across plausible states. Fixed Type I error control is one criterion and need not minimize losses from actual decisions.

**Inspected:** Introduction and §§2.1–2.3 inspected, pp. 2827–2833. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E07: The Limits of Antitrust

Frank H. Easterbrook (1984). Texas Law Review 63:1–40.

[Source](https://chicagounbound.uchicago.edu/journal_articles/1153/). The defense of restrained intervention relies partly on persistence of erroneous precedent and market correction of monopoly. Treat these as substantive claims to assess in a particular institutional setting.

**Inspected:** Opening argument, pp. 1–4, inspected. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E08: Taking the Error Out of “Error Cost” Analysis: What’s Wrong with Antitrust’s Right

Jonathan B. Baker (2015). Antitrust Law Journal 80(1):1–38; saved copy includes 2016 seminar cover.

[Source](https://digitalcommons.wcl.american.edu/facsch_lawrev/1356/). Assumptions about self-correction, courts, and other institutions can systematically tilt a decision framework toward nonintervention. The analysis directly contests one-sided treatment of error costs.

**Inspected:** Abstract and opening critique, pp. 1–5, inspected. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E09: Burden of Proof

Louis Kaplow (2012). Yale Law Journal 121:738–859.

[Source](https://yalelawjournal.org/article/burden-of-proof). Legal burdens also shape deterrence, beneficial activity, and enforcement incentives. A static posterior cutoff is an incomplete model of legal institutions.

**Inspected:** Abstract and opening introduction, p. 741, inspected; complete article downloaded. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E13: Merger Guidelines

U.S. Department of Justice; Federal Trade Commission (2023). Issued December 18, 2023; agency overview and §4.1 checked November 27, 2026.

[Source](https://www.justice.gov/atr/merger-guidelines/overview). The agencies consider the totality of evidence and risks to competition. Guidelines state agency practice, are not binding law, and concern merger review rather than every form of retrospective liability.

**Inspected:** Official overview, evidence guidance, and guideline document inspected. **Copy:** linked.

### E14: Communication from the Commission on the precautionary principle

European Commission (2000). COM(2000)1 final, February 2, 2000; European Union policy communication.

[Source](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=COM%3A2000%3A0001%3AFIN). Identified potential dangers and scientific uncertainty can motivate proportionate measures. The communication also requires consistency, examination of action and inaction, and review. It is not a general United States antitrust rule.

**Inspected:** Summary and proportionality/review criteria inspected. **Copy:** linked.

### E15: Verizon Communications Inc. v. Law Offices of Curtis V. Trinko, LLP

Supreme Court of the United States (2004). 540 U.S. 398, 407 (2004); U.S. federal monopolization.

[Source](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/02-682). Monopoly pricing alone does not establish unlawful monopolization. Identify the conduct and statutory elements separately from an estimated markup or moral objection.

**Inspected:** Existing course legal assessment and opinion passage on monopoly power and exclusionary conduct. **Copy:** linked.

## Philosophy and Concepts

### E10: Inductive Risk and Values in Science

Heather Douglas (2000). Philosophy of Science 67(4):559–579.

[Source](https://www.stephanieruphy.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Douglas-2000.pdf). Non-epistemic consequences of error can matter within scientific reasoning. The claim motivates transparency about stakes without allowing values to substitute for evidence.

**Inspected:** Opening argument inspected; original already held in the Data, Truth, and Norms collection. **Copy:** existing local copy.

### E11: Engineering Concepts? Tools for Social Change

Sally Haslanger (2022). Lecture handout, June 9, 2022.

[Source](https://sallyhaslanger.weebly.com/uploads/1/8/2/7/18272031/haslanger_san_raffaele_4_hdo.pdf). Concepts organize attention and coordinated responses. The guide applies that account to standards defining harm and admissible evidence; the source does not itself make an antitrust finding.

**Inspected:** Four-page handout inspected. **Copy:** downloaded.

### E12: What We Owe to Each Other

T. M. Scanlon (1998). Chapter 5, §§1–3, especially pp. 189–196.

[Source](https://e-edu.nbu.bg/pluginfile.php/314831/mod_resource/content/0/scanlon_5.1-3.pdf). Compare the reasons individuals have to reject alternative principles. Expected aggregate losses do not exhaust the justification owed to a person who bears a serious burden.

**Inspected:** Existing course excerpt and associated source assessment; no new download. **Copy:** existing local copy.

### E17: Sliced Cheese Data and Account-Level Modeling for Trade Promotion

Peter Boatwright; Robert McCulloch; Peter Rossi; bayesm package maintainers (1999; package 2025). bayesm 3.1-7, commit 453ae30857cf09a0c537f4deab64ed193493c8c9.

[Source](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/bayesm/html/cheese.html). Reanalysis of released data with exact mixture integration; fit, mechanism attribution, and permission remain distinct.

**Inspected:** Public R object, package documentation and license, and January 1999 preprint data/model description inspected. Public file has 88 accounts; preprint describes 77. **Copy:** public data downloaded.

### E18: Why We (Usually) Don't Have to Worry About Multiple Comparisons

Andrew Gelman; Jennifer Hill; Masanao Yajima (2012). Journal of Research on Educational Effectiveness 5:189–211.

[Source](https://doi.org/10.1080/19345747.2011.618213). Posterior means can shrink faster than their standard deviations, reducing standardized comparisons. Evaluate both missed harms and false flags.

**Inspected:** Abstract and algebra, pp. 196–198, inspected in full text; source supports the partial-pooling mechanism, not the course consumer-harm scenario. **Copy:** downloaded; Dropbox only.

### E19: Contractualism and Intuitionism

T. M. Scanlon (2025). Circulated NYU colloquium paper, 2025.

[Source](https://www.law.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/2025-09/T.%20M.%20Scanlon%2C%20Contractualism%20and%20Intuitionism.pdf). The relevant personal reasons of affected parties and reconsidered aggregation qualify a strictly bilateral objection comparison.

**Inspected:** Opening and printed pp. 40–43 inspected; circulated version, not a claim of final book publication. **Copy:** downloaded; Dropbox only.
