# Sources, Rights, and Review Scope

Checked November 26, 2026. Original annotations, simulations, calculations, and analytical figures were written for the course. No third-party code was executed.

## Statistical Sources

Lakens's *Improving Your Statistical Inferences*, Chapters 1 and 9, were read for p-value interpretation, repeated-experiment distributions, equivalence, interval hypotheses, and threshold justification. The book identifies CC BY-NC-SA licensing in its [preface](https://lakens.github.io/statistical_inferences/). The course links the chapters and uses independently written Python and original examples. It does not mirror the book or its figures.

## Electricity Paper and Communication

Rana, Kaps, and Netessine's 42-page October 2026 working-paper version was read, including the methods, results, discussion, and Appendix A/B tables. The reading copy was already available locally and is preserved unchanged. Its [SSRN record](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7580459) identifies Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licensing. The file hash and author order are recorded in `manifest.json`. Public full-page retrieval of SSRN was restricted, while indexed metadata confirmed its license and date. The paper's original utility panel and analysis code were not available in this review.

The HBS article was read directly. Its statement about four large firms is academic public communication. Its future-price qualification is retained. A targeted indexed-web search for company posts and a direct X search did not establish company dissemination; the X browser required authentication. Course notes identify the verified speaker and do not attribute a post to a company without evidence.

[Apple's Environmental Progress Report 2026](https://www.apple.com/environment/pdf/Apple_Environmental_Progress_Report_2026.pdf) was checked at pp. 95 and 99, which distinguish owned data centers from colocation. This supports the conceptual warning about inferring a facility business model from an owner-list complement. It does not establish which specific Apple facilities enter the paper's estimation sample. The report is linked, not redistributed.

## Philosophical Sources

Kant's *Groundwork*, second section, supplies the universal-law and humanity formulations. The public-domain translation is already held in the course's Marketing Dialectics collection. Thomasson's *Norms and Necessity* (2020) distinguishes requirements, permissions, and reports about rules; the checked publisher record and existing passage notes support the focused course application. *Rethinking Metaphysics*, Chapter 9 (2025), supplies functional assessment of concepts; its publisher abstract and existing notes were consulted. No claim of a new full-book reading is made.

The agency application distinguishes observed conduct, endorsement, and authority; it also distinguishes aggregate fit from identification of a process. Instructor-supplied conceptual notes informed that application. Those notes, unpublished manuscripts, and private research data are not included or linked in the public course repository. Third-party philosophers retain attribution. Confucian concepts refer to the existing primary-text discussions in M9 §§9.10–9.11.

## Scope of Verification

Published coefficients were visually checked against Tables 2, 4, and 7. The program calculates normal-approximation intervals and sensitivity at fixed reported standard errors. It does not re-estimate synthetic controls, recover coefficient covariance, identify household incidence, or establish actual policy harm. The critique distinguishes demonstrated interpretation problems from design concerns that require replication.
