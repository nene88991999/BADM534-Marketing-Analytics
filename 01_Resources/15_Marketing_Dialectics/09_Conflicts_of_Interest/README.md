# Conflicts of Interest, Attention, and Consumer Authority

A commercial objective can influence the question an analyst asks before it influences an estimate. These readings distinguish opposing interests, duties governing judgment, disclosure, dependence, reward learning, and the justification of a proposed action. The course application is in M9 §§9.45–9.49, with connections in M1 §1.22, M7 §7.47, and M8 §8.33.

The [Lindström et al. PDF](LindstromBellanderSchultnerChangToblerAmodio_NatureCommunications2021.pdf) is an unchanged author-hosted copy, shared under [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Copyright remains with the authors. Its final page states the license and the exception for separately credited third-party content. The other readings remain linked because accessibility alone does not establish redistribution rights. [sources.json](sources.json) records the retrieval and local-file hash.

This Markdown guide is a selective companion. It omits full derivations, article figures, appendices, and bibliographies. It distinguishes neuroscience, behavioral reward learning, and economic self-control evidence. None of these sources alone establishes that a particular interface causes clinical addiction.

## Course Use

The [original synthetic comparison](../../../03_Data_and_Code/Conflicts_of_Interest/README.md) contrasts commercial ranking with customer outcomes and a separately defended stopping requirement. The [optional lab](../../../07_Labs/Conflicts_of_Interest/README.md) asks students to identify a duty, construct an objection from an affected person's position, and revise a recommendation when a premise changes. No source data or empirical coefficient is used in the synthetic calculation.
