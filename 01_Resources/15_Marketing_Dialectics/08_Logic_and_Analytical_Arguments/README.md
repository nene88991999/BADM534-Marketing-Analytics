## Six Applications

1. **Compatibility and identification:** construct two models that fit the same historical law and disagree about the policy effect. State the evidence or restriction needed to distinguish them.
2. **Question formulation:** separate predicting acceptance, estimating an intervention effect, identifying a burden, and justifying a practice.
3. **Meaning and measurement:** preserve the distinction between purchase and consent, willingness to pay and benefit, unit price and household bill, and a formal option and an effective ability to use it.
4. **Counterfactual specification:** identify what changes and what remains fixed when pricing, disclosure, availability, or customer control changes.
5. **Valuation sensitivity:** hold purchase evidence and rank order fixed while varying independently supplied cardinal values. Attribute the resulting welfare change to that premise.
6. **Symmetric scrutiny:** require the criticism to preserve the source's actual claim and qualifications. An unsupported harmlessness inference leaves actual harm to be established separately.

## Phenomena, Noumena, and Acknowledged Epistemic Limits

M9 §9.41 and the opening of M1 distinguish empirical representation, unmeasured empirical questions, and the practical standing of affected people. The negative concept of noumenon (Kant, 1781/1787) limits theoretical claims about things considered independently of the conditions of sensible cognition. The course separately draws on the practical account of respect (Kant, 1785) and the intelligible standpoint. Qualitative evidence and latent empirical variables remain within possible empirical inquiry.

- [*Critique of Pure Reason*, chapter on phenomena and noumena](../01_Kant_Primary_Texts/public_domain_texts/Kant_CritiqueOfPureReason_Meiklejohn1855.html), especially B306–B311. Read the existing Meiklejohn text and checked the A/B locators against the [Kemp Smith translation](https://humanum.arts.cuhk.edu.hk/humftp/E-text/Kant/cpr.kemp-smith/11ph-nou.htm).
- [*Groundwork*, sections II–III](../01_Kant_Primary_Texts/public_domain_texts/Kant_Groundwork_Abbott.html), humanity at 4:428–429 and the two standpoints at 4:451–453. Read the existing Abbott text. These practical arguments retain their distinct role from the first Critique's theoretical limit.

The “known to be the never known” is treated as an acknowledged epistemic limit whose scope requires justification. It differs from an unanticipated empirical issue. A statistical non-identification construction gives a conditional limit under a specified observable law; a changed design can remove that limit. Normative questions remain open to reasoned justification. They are not classified as permanently unknowable merely because statistics do not settle them.
