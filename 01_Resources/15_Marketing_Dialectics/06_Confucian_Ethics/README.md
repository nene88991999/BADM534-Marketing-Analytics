## C01: The Analects of Confucius

Confucius and his disciples. ancient. James Legge translation; public-domain edition

Righteousness, trustworthiness, fitting conduct, truthful challenge, reciprocity, and reflective learning inform the marketing applications. Section numbering can differ between editions; consult the passage as well as its number.

**Reading scope:** Analects 2.15, 4.5, 4.16, 12.7, 13.3, 13.23, 14.22, 15.24; modern numbering compared with C02.
**Source:** [The Analects of Confucius](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/3330)
**Access:** full; downloaded.
**Rights:** public_domain_US.
**File:** [Confucius_Analects_Legge.html](public_domain_texts/Confucius_Analects_Legge.html).

## C02: The Analects of Confucius: An Online Teaching Translation

Confucius; translated and annotated by Robert Eno. 2015. University-hosted teaching translation; compare with Legge

Annotated modern teaching translation. Compare wording and explanatory notes with Legge’s historical translation; translation and commentary are distinct from the original text.

**Reading scope:** Introduction and selected passages listed under C01.

## C03: Mencius: An Online Teaching Translation

Mencius; translated and annotated by Robert Eno. 2016. University-hosted teaching translation

The opening challenges an exclusive focus on advantage; the account of moral beginnings emphasizes cultivable capacities. These political and ethical arguments require explicit interpretation when applied to firms.

**Reading scope:** Mencius 1A.1 and 2A.6 with surrounding discussion.
**Source:** [Mencius: An Online Teaching Translation](https://scholarworks.iu.edu/iuswrrest/api/core/bitstreams/265d73a0-6bfa-45df-92ff-4e7d3f8be4b1/content)

## C04: Selection from the Xunzi: Human Nature Is Evil

Xunzi. 1999. Excerpt from Sources of Chinese Tradition, compiled by Wm. Theodore de Bary and Irene Bloom, 2nd edition, volume 1 (1999), pp. 179–183

Unchecked desires and deliberate cultivation are central to Xunzi’s argument. Good conduct requires learning and patterned practice; this does not establish guilt in each particular transaction.

**Reading scope:** Longer selection from chapter 23.
**Source:** [Selection from the Xunzi: Human Nature Is Evil](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_china-xunzi-human-nature.htm)
**Access:** excerpt; downloaded.

## C06: Xunzi

Paul R. Goldin. 2025. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; live edition, copyright 2025

Clarifies Xunzi’s account and disagreements with Mencius, including differences in what human nature denotes. Moral cultivation remains possible and consequential.

**Reading scope:** Human nature and cultivation discussion.
