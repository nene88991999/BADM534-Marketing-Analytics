# Search Scope and Retrieval Record

Search and retrieval date: November 6, 2026. The scope began with Kantian dialectic and was extended at the instructor's request to philosophical scrutiny of corporate self-interest and Confucian insights. No date restriction was applied to classical texts. Contemporary applications were selected for a direct connection to marketing claims, customer agency, measurement, or conduct.

## Search Routes

- Primary Kant works and translations: Project Gutenberg, Early Modern Texts, and university teaching pages. Queries covered transcendental dialectic, antinomies, practical dialectic, regulative reason, Groundwork, and enlightenment.
- Interpretations: Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, author and university repositories, Cambridge and Yale publisher records, and Gava's open-access article. Bibliographic leads included Grier, Bennett, Willaschek, Allison, Kraus, and Bowie.
- Marketing: searches for Kantian business ethics, normative marketing ethics, advertising and autonomy, randomized advertising measurement, and consumer-interface experiments. Publisher and university versions were preferred.
- Practice: AMA ethics statement, FTC staff report, Google Ads documentation, Netflix's technical account, and both specified ASA rulings. Case outcomes were checked against each regulator's assessment.
- Incentives: Hume's *Of the Independency of Parliament* and the concluding discussion in Book I, chapter XI of Smith's *Wealth of Nations*.
- Confucian thought: Legge and Eno translations of the Analects, Eno's Mencius, Columbia's Xunzi excerpt, and specialist encyclopedia entries. The selected passages address righteousness and gain, trustworthy conduct, naming and roles, remonstrance, reciprocity, learning, and moral cultivation.

## Inclusion and Stopping Rules

Include identifiable primary texts, major interpretive guides, directly relevant research, and first-party institutional accounts. Record an unavailable work when it is central to the bibliography. Do not substitute a similarly titled thesis, review, front matter, or abstract for the requested work. Search-engine snippets identify leads; substantive course claims use the actual source passages described in the catalog's reading scope.

This is a curated teaching collection. It does not exhaust the German-language Kant literature, the history of dialectic, all editions of classical Chinese texts, or contemporary Confucian business ethics. Each broader literature is open-ended. Full books with incomplete access remain explicit in [ACCESS_GAPS.md](ACCESS_GAPS.md); no claim of comprehensive full-text retrieval is made.

## File Checks

Downloads were checked for HTTP success and expected PDF or HTML content. PDF files were parsed and page counts recorded. HTML challenge responses were rejected; publisher abstract pages were labeled as such. Files carry SHA-256 hashes to identify the retrieved version. These checks establish file identity and availability; they do not mean every page of every book was read.

No downloaded program was installed or executed. The numerical course example is original Python using synthetic records and the standard library. Credentials, student records, and private instructor papers are absent from the public collection.
