# Ontologies and Marketing Evidence

A marketing team needs a shared definition of a customer, purchase, cancellation, and consent before it can compare results across systems or ask an AI assistant to analyze them. An ontology makes categories, relationships, and inference rules explicit. Its value for this course is greatest when a plausible calculation conceals a disagreement about what the records mean.

The [Ontology Playground](https://learn-ontology.vercel.app/#learn) provides an accessible introduction through short lessons, editable graphs, and questions with reasoning traces. The strongest course adaptation is an exercise in specifying and challenging definitions. The [source review](SOURCE_REVIEW.md) separates the site's instructional ideas from the conditions needed for a reliable marketing application.

| Course Connection | Teaching Use |
| --- | --- |
| M1: Data Contracts and AI-Assisted Coding | Define entities, identifiers, events, relationships, units, and evidence before generating code |
| M2: Customer Data and Measurement | Distinguish a missing record, a recorded zero, an inferred category, and an independently verified outcome |
| M5: Text and Networks | Represent a speaker's claim with its source, date, and scope; evaluate retrieval against the original passage |
| M9: Marketing Dialectics | Examine who chose a category and whether the inference from a commercial record to a customer right is justified |
| Future Wiki | Let students predict an inference, reveal its supporting facts, change an assumption, and compare the result |
