## T03. Replies to Authors

(Thomasson, n.d.). Undated author manuscript; saved November 26, 2026. [Source](https://www.amiethomasson.org/_files/ugd/63fc8a_924e5fc0c3224895948994030f976cdb.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Manuscript pp. 15–20, especially modal normativism on pp. 19–20.

Distinguish the function of a discourse from its subject matter and truth conditions. Rules governing modal vocabulary do not imply that modal truths are made true by linguistic conventions.

**Access and scope:** Author manuscript downloaded; fiction, fact-talk, and modal-normativism passages inspected. Empirical knowledge remains necessary for many applications of concepts.

## T08. Inductive Risk and Values in Science

(Douglas, 2000). 2000; Philosophy of Science 67(4), 559–579. [Source](https://www.stephanieruphy.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Douglas-2000.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Consequences of error and direct versus indirect roles for values.

Consequences of mistaken acceptance or rejection can matter to evidentiary judgment. This supports examining the cost of public reassurance under uncertainty; it does not permit inventing an adverse result.

**Access and scope:** Full paper downloaded; inductive-risk argument and values discussion inspected.

## T09. How Values Can Be Good for Science

(Longino, 2004). 2004; Science, Values, and Objectivity, pp. 127–142. [Source](https://www.ugr.es/~zink/mono/Longino2004.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Critical interaction, pp. 134–135.

Objectivity requires effective criticism: venues, uptake, public standards, and tempered equality of intellectual authority. Merely permitting comments does little when residents cannot obtain evidence or influence the response.

**Access and scope:** Full chapter downloaded; opening and critical-interaction conditions inspected. The electricity application is the course’s argument.

## T12. Is Logic a Normative Discipline?

(MacFarlane, 2017). Author manuscript saved November 26, 2026; originally presented 2017. [Source](https://johnmacfarlane.net/normative.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Normative principles and the bridge to prescriptions.

An inference’s logical relation and the prescription governing a reasoner require careful articulation. In the course application, a valid statistical calculation does not supply the moral premise needed for a decision.

**Access and scope:** Full author manuscript downloaded; opening argument inspected. The course does not attribute a blanket denial of logical normativity to the argument (MacFarlane, 2017).

## T14. Epistemic Values and the Argument from Inductive Risk

(Steel, 2010). 2010; Philosophy of Science 77(1), 14–34; DOI 10.1086/650206. [Source](https://doi.org/10.1086/650206).

**Reading focus:** Epistemic values and legitimate value influence.

Assess how values can guide inquiry without replacing evidence. Pair with the inductive-risk argument (Douglas, 2000) when distinguishing the stakes of an error from reasons to believe a factual claim.

**Access and scope:** Publisher abstract inspected; full text not downloaded.

## T17. Truth

(Glanzberg, 2025). Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy; revision June 27, 2025. [Source](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/truth/).

**Reading focus:** Sections 1, 4–6: truth, evidence, language, and assertion.

Compare correspondence, coherence, pragmatist, and deflationary approaches without equating truth with convenience, consensus, or institutional approval. These theories differ; none is established merely by possessing a dataset.

**Access and scope:** Reference overview and correspondence discussion inspected; entry supplies routes to primary philosophical debates.
