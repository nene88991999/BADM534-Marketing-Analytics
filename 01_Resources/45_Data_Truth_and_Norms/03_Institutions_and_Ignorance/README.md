## T07. Introduction to “Raw Data” Is an Oxymoron

(Gitelman and Jackson, 2013). 2013; edited by Lisa Gitelman. [Source](https://dsl.lsu.edu/nehtextualdata/wp-content/uploads/2017/11/RawData.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Background reading on selection and preparation of data.

The title provides a useful question: which decisions preceded the supposedly raw file? Use the inspected readings on data practices (Leonelli, 2015, 2020) for the developed account of data practices.

**Access and scope:** Bibliographic lead only; download timed out twice. No chapter-level summary or full-text reading claim.

## T10. The Problem of Undone Science: Values, Interests, and the Selection of Research Programs

(Hess, 1998). 1998 unpublished conference paper. [Source](https://www.davidjhess.net/uploads/3/4/8/1/34811322/problemundone19984s.formatted.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Historical formulation of undone science.

Research agendas can omit questions important to less powerful constituencies. Pair this historical statement with the later analysis of agenda setting (Frickel et al., 2010).

**Access and scope:** Author copy downloaded; opening and agenda-selection argument inspected. Historical medical examples are outside the course application and are not endorsed.

## T11. What Gets Counted Counts

(D’Ignazio and Klein, 2020). 2020; Data Feminism, Chapter 4. [Source](https://data-feminism.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/h1w0nbqp/release/3).

**Reading focus:** Classification, exclusions, and the power to define categories.

A schema can impose categories that misrepresent the people recorded. Assess who chose the categories and whose experiences disappear; counting can also support accountability and collective claims.

**Access and scope:** Public chapter HTML and opening examples inspected; MIT-hosted 34-page PDF downloaded. PDF states CC BY 4.0.

## T13. The Logic of Strategic Ignorance

(McGoey, 2012). 2012; British Journal of Sociology; DOI 10.1111/j.1468-4446.2012.01424.x. [Source](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/5131/).

**Reading focus:** Ignorance as an organizational resource.

Investigate when not knowing, or being able to claim not to know, benefits an institution. A missing public field is insufficient to establish intentional suppression.

**Access and scope:** Repository abstract inspected; repository reports full text unavailable. No local full-text copy.

## T15. Undone Science: Charting Social Movement and Civil Society Challenges to Research Agenda Setting

Scott Frickel, Sahra Gibbon, Jeff Howard, Joanna Kempner, Gwen Ottinger, and (Hess, 1998). 2010; Science, Technology, & Human Values 35(4), 444–473; DOI 10.1177/0162243909345836. [Source](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/249623643_Undone_Science_Charting_Social_Movement_and_Civil_Society_Challenges_to_Research_Agenda_Setting).

**Reading focus:** Civil-society challenges to research priorities.

Questions can remain unasked, unfunded, or incomplete through institutional priorities. Household cost incidence may require research infrastructure that routine commercial accounting does not provide.

**Access and scope:** Author-uploaded article HTML; abstract and introductory argument inspected. No local PDF.
