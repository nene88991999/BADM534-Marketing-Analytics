# Confidentiality, Research Independence, and Public Claims

The research decision concerns whether access to a valuable dataset justifies the conditions attached to it. Distinguish an agreement that protects records from one that governs permissible questions or publication. The actual terms and their implementation determine the constraint. The label NDA alone supplies little information about those terms.

## Artifactual Interpretation

The file's schema and contents form one part of the research object. Its governed use concerns who may inspect it, which analyses are permitted, who can correct it, and which results can become public. Shared norms can help constitute recognized artifacts without making each object publicly accessible (Thomasson, 2014, §5.4). Applying this distinction to research asks how a contract and professional practice shape what the published paper represents.

A restriction on identifiers can protect people. A restriction on questions can limit which burdens receive scrutiny. A discretionary publication right can select the visible literature after an otherwise valid analysis. These mechanisms differ, and an allegation that one operated in a named study requires evidence. Unknown restrictions leave uncertainty about the reporting process; they do not reveal an unpublished effect.

## Teaching Application

[M9 §§9.63–9.65](../../../06_Lecture_Notes/modules/M09_Marketing_Dialectics.md) compares three hypothetical agreements using the same six-account synthetic history. Students distinguish confidentiality review, restricted research purpose, and discretionary sponsor approval. The [optional practice](../../../07_Labs/Data_Truth_and_Norms/README.md) asks whether NDA-governed research ought to exist, separating the decision to conduct a study from publication and public reliance.

A defensible answer addresses affected people's reasons, privacy, the value of knowledge, the risk of selective reassurance, and feasible alternative terms. It identifies conditions for proceeding, renegotiating, or declining. A researcher can have a reason to forgo a publication when the governing arrangement prevents adequate justification to the people affected by its use.
