# Household Electricity Evidence and Access

[Collection Guide](../README.md) | [Catalog](../catalog.json)

## E01. 2020 RECS Microdata

U.S. Energy Information Administration. Expenditure release June 2023; file update January 2024. [Source](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/data/2020/index.php?view=microdata).

**Reading focus:** Public household consumption and expenditure files and documentation.

Public household expenditure microdata already exist. They do not supply the longitudinal project-to-household linkage or counterfactual needed to identify a particular data center’s cost incidence.

**Access and scope:** Official page and release information inspected. No RECS estimates are calculated in the synthetic exercise.

## E02. Residential Energy Consumption Survey: Frequently Asked Questions

U.S. Energy Information Administration. Current page checked November 26, 2026. [Source](https://www.eia.gov/consumption/residential/faqs.php).

**Reading focus:** Population, supplier billing data, modeling, and weights.

EIA collects energy-supplier billing data after the household survey. Its target is occupied primary residences; some outputs involve modeling and calibration. The complex survey design governs estimation.

**Access and scope:** Official FAQ inspected. Separate observed bills, modeled end uses, and public-file disclosure restrictions.

## E03. CIPSEA Statistical Data Access

U.S. Energy Information Administration. Current page checked November 26, 2026. [Source](https://www.eia.gov/about/cipsea_reporting.php).

**Reading focus:** Confidentiality and the restricted-data application process.

Restricted research access is an institutional alternative to open release. Privacy protections and application requirements constrain what can be studied and by whom.

**Access and scope:** Official access page inspected. Eligibility, approval, and available files must be established for a specific project; no access was requested.

## E04. Green Button

U.S. Department of Energy. Program page checked November 26, 2026. [Source](https://www.energy.gov/data/green-button).

**Reading focus:** Customer access and authorized sharing of energy data.

Customer-authorized access offers one route to evidence. Availability and detail vary; consent-based samples can still select unusually concerned or well-resourced households.

**Access and scope:** Official program description inspected. Historical adoption figures are not treated as current counts or universal access.

## E05. Data Centers in Virginia

Virginia Joint Legislative Audit and Review Commission. December 9, 2024. [Source](https://jlarc.virginia.gov/pdfs/reports/Rpt598.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Public investigation of data-center infrastructure and electricity costs.

Political concern can generate official inquiry and public analysis. Forecasts and cost-allocation analysis must retain their scenario assumptions and differ from realized household causal effects.

**Access and scope:** Full official report downloaded; scope and electricity-cost discussion inspected. No forecast is presented as an observed household treatment effect.

## E06. Power and Profits: How the AI Data Center Boom Costs Households and Communities

Offices of Senators Elizabeth Warren, Chris Van Hollen, and Richard Blumenthal. October 2026. [Source](https://www.warren.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/471703-ai-data-center-boom-cost-Digital-8.5x11.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Report pp. 8–9: nondisclosure practices and differences among respondents.

A Senate investigation describes contractual secrecy and disagreement over cost responsibilities. Compare its interpretation with the underlying company responses and regulatory records.

**Access and scope:** Full report downloaded; nondisclosure discussion inspected. Attribute claims to the investigating senators. This later document does not establish exposure of particular 2020–2025 sample units.

## E07. Responses to Senators on Data-Center Costs

Responding data-center companies. January 2026 correspondence. [Source](https://www.warren.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/media/doc/big_tech_responses.pdf).

**Reading focus:** Google letter, pp. 2–3 of the bundle; confidentiality and regulatory exceptions.

The company describes bespoke contract terms as generally confidential while recognizing regulatory review and legally required disclosure. This directly documents selective access to particular contracts; it does not show that household bills are unrecorded.

**Access and scope:** Full 35-page bundle downloaded; specified letter passages inspected. Company assurances about protections require independent evaluation.

## E08. S. 5640: Data Center Transparency Act of 2026

U.S. Senate. Introduced September 30, 2026; introduced text checked November 26, 2026. [Source](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/BILLS-119s5640is/xhtml/BILLS-119s5640is.html).

**Reading focus:** Section 2(c): collection and public electricity reports.

The introduced federal bill proposes reporting on data-center electricity use, grid buildout, and household energy bills and costs. It demonstrates a demand for disclosure; reported changes would still require causal interpretation.

**Access and scope:** Official introduced text inspected. A proposal is not an operative reporting duty. No enactment or effective date is inferred; later legislative action was not verified.
