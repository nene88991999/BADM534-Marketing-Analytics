## Moral Comparison

An identified household can suffer exposure of intimate consumption. A firm may instead seek to protect commercial relationships, its reputation, or a valuable information product. Those interests have different grounds. Their inclusion in a single confidentiality regime does not establish that they deserve the same protection.

The strongest objection concerns accountability: a valid aggregate finding may remain difficult to connect to a responsible firm or use in an adversarial setting. That is a consequence to assess against a defended benefit of confidentiality. It is not proof that a particular unlawful practice occurred or that an adverse manuscript was suppressed. Advance compliance review establishes an intervention point; a claim about an actual veto needs its terms and correspondence.

The strongest defense concerns participation, legitimate commercial interests, and research that would otherwise be unavailable. Ask which evidence supports that counterfactual and whether protected scrutiny can preserve the benefit at a lower cost to affected people. Possible alternatives include independent secure access, bounded disclosure review, and a protected route for competent authorities. These alternatives are normative proposals, not representations of existing access rights or legal advice.

## Responsibility and Maxims

Reconstruct the rule governing the restriction. A maxim aimed at protecting identifiable people differs from one aimed at insulating a firm from supported criticism. Actual purposes require evidence. Evaluate what the rule permits, who can contest it, and which actor has authority to change it. A provider's disclaimer about responsibility for conclusions can coexist with upstream control of the artifact.

For a research recommendation, report the protected interest, restricted action, independent access route, publication authority, and residual evidentiary gap. If a gap is decisive for a public assurance, narrow reliance on that assurance. Research without an NDA still needs accessible records, provenance, and a defensible design.
