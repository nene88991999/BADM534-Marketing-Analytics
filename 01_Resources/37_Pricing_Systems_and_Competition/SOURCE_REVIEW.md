# Evidence for Pricing Systems and Competition

## The Initial Complaint

**PC02.** *Garcia v. The Wendy's Company and NCR Voyix Corporation*, No. 8:26-cv-04886-LKG, U.S. District Court for the District of Maryland, Document 1, filed November 23, 2026, 26 pages. [Complaint](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.mdd.618524/gov.uscourts.mdd.618524.1.0.pdf). [Docket](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/75164297/garcia-v-the-wendys-company/).

Paragraphs 5–13 and 42–51 connect required technology, transaction data, and pricing announcements to alleged coordination. Paragraphs 71–83 plead Sherman Act Section 1 claims concerning price fixing and information exchange. These are the plaintiff's factual allegations and legal arguments. The filing requests class certification, damages, and injunctive relief; it supplies no judicial finding on the merits.

Paragraph 2 defines the proposed class period from January 1, 2022. Paragraphs 73 and 79 allege conduct beginning no earlier than 2016 and no later than 2022 and continuing thereafter. Describing the allegation as a completed 2016–2022 scheme would misstate that timing. Paragraphs 30–33 offer a product and geographic market theory; that theory also remains the plaintiff's position.

The complaint cites public documents and interprets them as evidence of coordination. Paragraph 51 relies on pricing announcements; paragraphs 53–55 also discuss cost pressures and broad price increases. Assess the further evidence connecting these sources to actual pricing authority, interfirm agreement, and the prices that would have prevailed under an alternative arrangement. A price increase or a common vendor alone does not identify those mechanisms.

The company stated that it did not plan to raise prices during its busiest periods and described possible discounts and menu changes. This is a dated statement about planned features. It predates the 2026 lawsuit and is not a response to it. Digital display, time-varying pricing, personalized pricing, and an alleged interfirm agreement identify different objects.

## Economic Research and Its Scope

**PC05.** Joseph E. Harrington, Jr. (2022), [The Effect of Outsourcing Pricing Algorithms on Market Competition](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2021.4241), *Management Science*, 68(9), 6889–6906. [Author-hosted article](https://joeharrington5201922.github.io/pdf/ms22.pdf). Read the abstract, model setup, welfare analysis, and conclusion. Paragraphs 58–59 of the complaint cite this study; the article's actual page range is 6889–6906.

The paper models a developer choosing a pricing algorithm and firms choosing whether to adopt it. Under its assumptions, outsourcing increases sensitivity to demand variation, raises industry profit, and reduces consumer welfare while leaving competitive intensity unchanged. Those results do not establish coordination at Wendy's. The distinction is useful because a customer loss and a finding about competition require different reasoning.

The course's two-restaurant calculation is an original quadratic-demand example. It specifies a common noisy signal, symmetric firms, and two pricing objectives. It does not reproduce Harrington's vendor-design and adoption equilibrium, estimate restaurant demand, or quantify damages. The source supplies a conceptual comparison; the course program supplies independently defined assumptions and exact calculations.

## Agency Argument and Legal Status

**PC06.** U.S. Department of Justice and Federal Trade Commission, [Statement of Interest in *Cornish-Adebiyi v. Caesars Entertainment*](https://www.justice.gov/usdoj-media/opa/media/1345721/dl?inline=), No. 1:23-cv-02536-KMW-EAP (D.N.J.), Document 96, March 28, 2024. The first 14 pages are the statement; the 61-page PDF includes attached filings. Read pages 3–7 and 12–13.

The agencies argue that delegating pricing to a common intermediary can satisfy the concerted-action element and that an agreement about a starting price can remain actionable even when final prices differ. Their argument requires attention to the alleged agreement and the relevant competitors. It is a litigation submission in a separate case, with no effective date as a newly enacted rule and no holding about Wendy's. Do not describe an override button as a sufficient legal safe harbor or every shared pricing tool as unlawful.
