# Shared Pricing Systems and Customer Choice

## The Managerial Decision

A restaurant operator must decide which data to supply to a pricing provider and what authority to delegate. A director asks whether the expected profit increase comes from lower operating costs, better demand information, or reduced independent price competition. Each mechanism implies a different comparison and may affect customers differently.

## Discussion Sequence

Session 14 uses minutes 50–60 for the existing personalized-pricing benchmark and minutes 60–70 for the Wendy's complaint and source evidence. Minutes 80–90 apply Kantian and Confucian reasoning to the case; minutes 90–100 retain the other justification and customer-authority comparisons. Minutes 100–110 revisit the synthetic pricing calculation, followed by the existing pricing and renewal work through minute 130. The optional lab carries the full code exercise. Session 5 uses its existing 145–155 pricing block to introduce the numerical comparison.

1. Read the complaint's description of the data and pricing arrangement alongside the 2022 company announcement. Identify which statements concern capability, reported conduct, interpretation, and a legal conclusion.
2. Request the evidence that would discriminate between explanations: relevant franchise contracts, data permissions, the pricing objective, recommendations, actual prices, overrides, cost changes, promotions, ownership, and communications. Avoid asserting that these records have been obtained.
3. Compare the four endpoint rows in the synthetic market. Change the pricing objective while holding information fixed. Change signal accuracy while holding the objective fixed. Explain why the two comparisons answer different questions.
4. Evaluate a stated pricing maxim, the truthful explanation offered to customers and franchisees, feasible alternatives, and the process for challenging a harmful result. Require reasons beyond a commercial performance metric.
5. Write a conditional recommendation identifying the evidence that would change it. Separate empirical uncertainty, legal analysis, and philosophical judgment.

## Interpretation Questions

Why can the same average posted price accompany different customer surplus? What changes if restaurants sell unrelated products? Which results would change if the pricing service reduced costs? Who is omitted from a surplus calculation that includes only the two firms and their customers? Why does the ability to reject one offer leave a separate question about the quality of alternative offers?

The numerical example contains synthetic demand and no actual Wendy's price observations. The existing restaurant-availability dataset records equipment status and cannot establish this case's prices, agreement, or customer harm. A future empirical application would need appropriate pricing, cost, adoption, ownership, and customer-choice data.
