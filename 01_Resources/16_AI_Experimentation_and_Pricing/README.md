# AI Experimentation, Pricing, and Ethical Evaluation

Four research papers by Sanjog Misra and his coauthors connect AI-assisted experiments, personalized pricing, robustness, and segmentation to distinct managerial decisions. The course case evaluates their stated objectives, reported evidence, safeguards, and ethical questions. A commercial gain and an ethical failure require different evidence and arguments.

- [Research Papers](01_Research/README.md): versioned sources and precise reading locations.
- [Pricing Policy](02_Policy/README.md): current law, proposed policy, legislative status, and inquiries checked November 6, 2026.
- [Claim Assessment](CLAIM_ASSESSMENT.md): supported findings and the additional premises required for a critique.
- [Catalog](catalog.json): source URLs, saved versions, checksums, access status, and reading scope.
- [Marketing Dialectics, Sections 9.14–9.18](../../06_Lecture_Notes/modules/M09_Marketing_Dialectics.md): the detailed case and Kantian argument.
- [Reproducible Pricing Example](../../03_Data_and_Code/AI_Experimentation_and_Pricing/README.md): exact synthetic comparisons and arithmetic from published summaries.

The research PDFs and downloaded policy snapshots are reference copies kept in Dropbox. Source links and original course commentary are public. Three New York legislative pages were read online but could not be saved through direct download. The pricing paper is saved in its June 2021 NBER revision; selected published JPE tables and discussion were cross-checked online. The 2025 revision of Coarse Personalization is the teaching version; the earlier retrieved 2022 file remains an internal version record.

Kant's primary texts and philosophical scholarship are in the [Marketing Dialectics collection](../15_Marketing_Dialectics/README.md). This collection concerns Sanjog Misra; pricing-bandit papers by Kanishka Misra are separate works and are not attributed to him.
