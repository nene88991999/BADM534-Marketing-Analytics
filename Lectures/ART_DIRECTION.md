# Chinese Ink and Pale-Color Painting

Use Chinese ink-and-color painting as the basis for artistic illustrations. The visual references are historical landscape traditions, with calligraphic brushwork, restrained washes, and open space. The illustrations are original digital interpretations. Their modern objects connect the landscapes to marketing decisions.

## Brushwork and Washes

Build forms through varied strokes. An upright brush produces controlled lines with changes in pressure, speed, and direction. An oblique brush supplies broader masses. Keep some contours discontinuous so that the paper participates in the form. A dark tip and a wetter, lighter brush body can produce tonal variation within one stroke. The Asian Art Museum explains these distinctions in its [introduction to Chinese brushpainting techniques](https://education.asianart.org/resources/an-introduction-to-chinese-brushpainting-techniques/).

Use dry-brush breaks selectively on rocks, trunks, and foreground textures. The exposed paper within a stroke gives the *fei bai*, or flying-white, effect. Preserve a clear distinction between textured brushwork and soft atmospheric washes. Uniform blur or a grain overlay cannot supply the different functions of these marks. The museum's [Brushstrokes teaching packet](https://education.asianart.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/6/2019/09/Brushstrokes.pdf) provides examples of brushwork, materials, and landscape texture strokes.

## Space, Materials, and Color

Separate foreground, middle distance, and remote mountains with scale, ink density, and mist. Leave substantial areas of pale paper for water, sky, and intervals between forms. Moving viewpoints and multiple distances can guide the eye through a landscape. The Metropolitan Museum's discussion of [*Summer Mountains*](https://www.metmuseum.org/exhibitions/listings/2008/anatomy-of-a-masterpiece/photo-gallery) connects brushwork, atmospheric space, and different kinds of distance.

Paper and silk support different effects, and sizing changes absorbency. Do not describe every traditional support as “rice paper.” These course illustrations use the appearance of lightly textured, warm paper with ink and pale color. Rich blue-green mineral landscapes are a different historical color treatment and should not be implied by every image.

Keep the main marks near Illinois Blue `#13294B`, with sparing Illini Orange `#FF5F05` accents. Allow atmospheric tints and warm paper rather than filling large areas with saturated orange. Quantitative figures use the exact Illinois palette and remain editable TikZ. Artistic illustrations contain no data labels, invented Chinese writing, decorative seals, or signatures.

## Composition and Use

Use a wide composition that reads from the back of a classroom. A merchant, boat, bridge, garden path, or ledger should make one consequential decision visible. Small laptops and tablets are deliberate contemporary elements. Keep objects integrated with the brushwork and preserve negative space around the focal action.

Art introduces a decision or mechanism; analytical figures establish the measured or imposed comparison. Give each illustration a text description in its Markdown companion. Keep a source and prompt record in [the artwork provenance](assets/PROVENANCE.md). Future artistic additions should follow the same brushwork, spacing, and palette conventions.
