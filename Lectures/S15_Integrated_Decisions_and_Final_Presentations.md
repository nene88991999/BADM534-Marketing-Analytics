- Connect validated analysis to a concrete marketing decision.

- Communicate a reproducible conclusion and a useful next test.

## Slide 2: An Integrated Marketing Decision

- Choose a feasible action and a defined customer population.
- Connect measurement, modeling, and evaluation to that action.
- State the costs, customer outcomes, and uncertainty that affect the choice.

The evidence chain can include a descriptive audit, a predictive model, a causal comparison, and a scenario. Each component should retain its own scope and assumptions.

## Slide 3: The Evidence Behind the Recommendation

- State the sample, observation unit, and outcome horizon.
- Show the comparison that supports the main claim.
- Explain which result changes the recommended action.

A result becomes decision-relevant when it changes a feasible choice under explicit costs or constraints. The audience should be able to assess the supporting comparison directly.

## Slide 4: A Calculation the Audience Can Check

- Trace one figure to an aggregate table and source records.
- Reproduce one prediction, coefficient interpretation, or policy value.
- Retain complete code and data definitions with the result.

A hand-checkable calculation complements complete reproducible code. It provides independent evidence about the unit, denominator, and direction of the computation.

## Slide 5: A Result That Could Change the Decision

- Identify the most consequential uncertain parameter.
- Show the threshold at which the preferred action changes.
- Consider a feasible test that could resolve that uncertainty.

Sensitivity should connect uncertainty to action. A wide parameter range is useful when it reveals whether the decision is stable or fragile.

## Slide 6: Customer Consequences

- Report effects on access, time, value, and unwanted charges where relevant.
- Distinguish voluntary continued use from difficulty leaving.
- Explain how customers can refuse or correct the proposed interaction.

The relevant customer outcome depends on the product and action. A higher platform metric can conceal costs outside the optimized objective.

## Slide 7: Regulatory Scope

- Identify the jurisdiction and covered conduct.
- Separate the current rule from a proposal or allegation.
- Match the evidence supporting a claim to what customers will understand.

The primary-source record should identify the effective date and consequential exclusions. A legal conclusion for one sector or jurisdiction should not be transferred mechanically to another.

## Slide 8: Presentation Discussion

- Is the recommendation supported for the stated population?
- What analytical or customer risk has the largest consequence?
- Which part of the result can be reproduced independently?

Feedback should name an observable gap in the evidence or a specific counterexample. The strongest response supplies a corrected comparison or explains why the original remains valid.

## Slide 9: A Deployment Comparison

- Retain a benchmark and a defined evaluation population.
- Monitor outcomes and data coverage after the action changes behavior.
- Set a response when quality or customer outcomes deteriorate.

A policy that changes exposure can also change future training data. Evaluation after deployment needs versioned inputs, outcomes, and policy rules.

## Slide 10: An Evidence-Based Recommendation

- Name the action, expected consequence, and decision threshold.
- Keep consequential uncertainty beside the estimate.
- Identify the next concrete extension supported by the evidence.

A conditional recommendation can be decisive when its conditions are measurable. The final decision should remain traceable to the data, assumptions, and customer outcomes.
