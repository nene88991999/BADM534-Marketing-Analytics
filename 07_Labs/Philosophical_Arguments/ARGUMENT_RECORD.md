# Analytical Argument Record

## Claim and Question

Record the exact claim, source passage and locator, actor, proposed action, population, comparison, period, and units. State the question answered by the analysis and the question the decision requires. Define consequential terms such as benefit, consent, household cost, and permission.

## Ends, Representation, and Standing

State who selected the objective and what authority permits pursuing it. Identify unmeasured empirical questions and the evidence that could address them. Explain any Kantian limit on treating the empirical representation as complete. Defend the practical principle concerning the affected person's standing; identify which end the firm ought to pursue, constrain, or abandon.

## Ontological Scope and Epistemic Limit

Place the particular inference on the spectrum in M9 §9.42 and explain the placement through a source passage. Identify the object omitted or substituted and why it changes the decision. Classify the unresolved issue as an empirical gap, an unanticipated issue now recognized, a conditional identification limit, a Kantian theoretical limit, or a question of practical justification. State the scope and reason for any impossibility claim. Preserve the source's safeguards and welfare findings.

## Counterexample and Missing Premise

Describe a case in which the stated premises hold and the conclusion fails. Check each premise against that case. Identify whether the construction is hypothetical or empirically supported, and identify the added premise that would exclude it. Explain why that premise deserves acceptance.

## Strongest Reply

State the strongest supported response to the criticism. Preserve the source's actual scope, positive findings, and qualifications. Check whether the objection establishes invalidity, unsupported interpretation, non-identification, a reporting inconsistency, or actual contrary evidence.

## Revised Conclusion and Further Evidence

State what the analysis supports and the evidence or reasons that would change the conclusion. For an ought claim, defend the normative principle, compare a feasible alternative, and assess affected people's reasons for rejecting each principle. Identify whose burdens or choices are missing from the analytical representation.
