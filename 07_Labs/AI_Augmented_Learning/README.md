# Learning Tools, Evidence, and Independent Judgment

This optional, ungraded exercise asks whether an instructional change helps a student understand and challenge an analytical argument. It establishes no assignment requirement or course-wide AI permission.

## Predict and Explain

Open the [electricity case](../../08_Wiki/pilot/index.html), or use its [complete text](../../08_Wiki/pilot/LESSON.md). Predict the average monthly bill when realized revenue remains USD 100/MWh and average monthly usage rises from 1 to 1.1 MWh. Explain why the unit-price statistic alone leaves the bill unresolved. Reveal the calculation, then state what further evidence would support a causal claim about a data center.

Change the price to USD 125/MWh and usage to 0.8 MWh per account-month. State how this comparison changes the measurement claim. Separate an accounting identity, an attribution of a change, and a judgment about affordability. Write a condition under which the original study would support a narrower reassuring conclusion.

## Compare Reported Magnitudes

Use [learning_comparisons.py](../../03_Data_and_Code/Learning_Design/learning_comparisons.py) to compare the rounded published percentages of 78% and 67%. Explain why they imply an 11-point difference and a relative increase of approximately 16.42%, identifying the denominator for each calculation. These arithmetic comparisons reconstruct no sampling uncertainty.

## Identify a Component Effect

Use the four synthetic means in the program. Compare the whole package with the separate contributions of questions and alternative representations. Explain why observing only the two diagonal cells leaves those contributions unresolved. Propose random assignment to four conditions, a common assessment, an appropriate time allowance, and a delayed transfer task. Define whether the target is the total effect of access or an effect under a fixed study duration.

## Challenge the Explanation

Write a claim, its measurement premise, its causal premise, and the normative premise behind a recommendation. Give the strongest supported objection and a reason for revising the conclusion. Compare an unfamiliar setting, such as a subscription with an unchanged monthly rate and higher total charges. Explain which parts of the electricity argument transfer and which need new institutional evidence.

When an applicable assessment permits AI, ask an assistant to produce an alternative explanation from an identified course passage. Check whether it preserves units, assumptions, contrary findings, and the distinction between possible and established harm. Keep the original passage accessible. Judge the explanation by the reasoning it supports and the errors it introduces.
