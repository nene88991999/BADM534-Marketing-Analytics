# Labs and Code

[Evidence Selection and AI Grader Evaluation](AI_Evidence_and_Evaluation/README.md) is available as public, ungraded practice. It includes two verified standard-library Python examples, fictional data, expected outputs, and an optional live-model protocol. It establishes no assignment requirements or course-wide AI policy.

Downloaded reference notebooks remain in `01_Resources`; their saved outputs do not certify a course-tested run. Each released lab should identify inputs, dependencies, commands, expected checks, provenance, and verification. Students should explain analytical choices and check small examples after using a coding assistant.

## Clustering Optimization

[Clustering Optimization and Interpretation](Clustering_Optimization/README.md) is available as optional, ungraded practice. A 15-minute discussion uses exact centroid arithmetic and saved CPU results; an optional Python run compares three implementations with common data, initial centers, and final scoring.

## Discount Response and Customer Outcomes

[Discount Response Curves and Customer Outcomes](Dose_Response/README.md) is optional, ungraded practice. A 20-minute discussion uses saved curve and uncertainty comparisons; optional Python work changes overlap, nuisance specifications, pooling, and the decision objective. All data are synthetic.

## Retail Access and Service Priorities

[Retail Accessibility](Retail_Accessibility/README.md) is optional ungraded practice using exact synthetic networks and four selected observed restaurant origins. Students compare paths, missing-status bounds, fixed-k graphs, cartographic footprints, and service objectives. Complete programs, input hashes, and independent checks accompany the exercise.

## Anomaly Detection and Diagnosis

[Retail diagnosis practice](Anomaly_Diagnosis/README.md) is optional and ungraded. Reproduce later-period alert metrics, inspect symptoms and causes, compare interval scores, preserve earlier information, and assess an explanation against disclosed synthetic incidents.

## Inference Efficiency and Customer Service

[Optional ungraded practice](Inference_Efficiency/README.md) reproduces a drafting-cost simulation and a return-service evaluation. Compare frozen choices, inspect EOS and rejected suffix costs, retain timeouts, and evaluate cost per correct automatic resolution.

## Training Mixtures and Customer Choice

[Training Data Mixtures](Training_Data_Mixtures/README.md) is optional ungraded practice accompanying M7 §§7.33–7.35 and Session 11. Students reproduce the ridge mechanism, change source similarity and penalty assumptions, preserve paired customer requests, and compare published final and selected checkpoints. The exercise connects independent calculations to evidence about cancellation, refunds, and customer choice.

## Synthetic Consumers and Product Screening

[Synthetic Consumer Practice](Synthetic_Consumers/README.md) is optional and ungraded. Students reproduce text-to-rating transformations, preserve persona weights, assess tied and reversed rankings, and design a prospective human evaluation that includes rejected concepts. All supplied data are invented, with no model account required.

## Reproducible Recommendations and Export

[Product-Screening Recommendations](Reproducible_Recommendations/README.md) is optional ungraded practice for M1 §1.15 and M8 §8.25. Students trace a recommendation to its inputs, preserve persona weights and undefined rankings, reproduce the calculation after moving files, and inspect exported equations and links. Python uses only the standard library; Suzuri and live model use are optional.

## Human and Synthetic Research Budgets

[Auxiliary Data Practice](Auxiliary_Data/README.md) is optional and ungraded. Students compare research cost at a common coefficient-MSE target, vary source noise, challenge an omitted bias restriction, and design independent human validation. The two standard-library programs require no model account or observed customer data.

## Graph Coarsening and Evaluation

[Optional ungraded practice](Graph_Coarsening/README.md) examines original-population access, group weights, oracle label purity, outcome-dependent preprocessing, and customer-service responsibilities. The exercise includes a source-inspection and AI-assisted coding specification.

## Definitions and Representation Quality

- [Cancellation Evidence](Ontology_and_Evidence/README.md): event definitions, cutoff information, duplicate records, validation, unknown states, and the review queue.
- [Representation Evaluation](Representation_Evaluation/README.md): reconstruction error, coordinate units, task recall, nonunique decompositions, and independent interpretation.

Both activities are public optional ungraded practice with original synthetic inputs and executed standard-library Python.

## Agent Workflow Evaluation

[Optional ungraded practice](Agent_Workflows/README.md) checks distinct logging days, roster denominators, date boundaries, customer-level errors, and total workflow cost. An optional four-configuration model comparison keeps development feedback separate from final evaluation.

## Representation Alignment

[Changing text representations in mHealth](Representation_Alignment/README.md) is public optional ungraded practice. Students hand-check a rotation, compare neighbor overlap and classification, reproduce a local mHealth evaluation, and justify the reference-label and cost comparisons. It supports M5 §5.26 and M7 §§7.42–7.44, with connections to M8 and M9.

## Pricing Systems and Customer Choice

[Pricing Information and Customer Outcomes](Pricing_Competition/README.md) is optional ungraded practice with full Python, synthetic parameters, exact results, and evidence questions. Compare independent and joint objectives at a fixed signal accuracy, then change information while retaining the objective. M4, M8, and M9 provide the derivation, Wendy's case, and ethical evaluation.

## Digital Usage and Customer Choice

The [optional ungraded lab](Digital_Usage/README.md) follows an app-service decision through data assessment, geographic maps, regression, clustering, index verification, and Kantian and Confucian evaluation.

## Statistical Evidence and Marketing Decisions

The [optional ungraded lab](Statistical_Inference/README.md) checks customer pairing, store assignment, uncertainty, Monte Carlo calibration, and the scope of an advertising claim. An optional recorded AI exercise evaluates execution, method, numerical reporting, and interpretation separately.

## Coding-Workflow Evidence and Constructed Errors

The expanded [AI Workflow Evaluation lab](Agent_Workflows/README.md) reconstructs published benchmark aggregates, assesses deliberate mHealth errors, and evaluates hypothetical review spending. Two separate optional model-comparison protocols distinguish controlled information/feedback contrasts from three execution-and-revision conditions. Neither has been run as a course model experiment.

## Statistical Claims, Household Burdens, and Agency

The [statistical-claims examples](Statistical_Inference/README.md) connect repeated p-values, economic thresholds, equivalence, and a recurring data-center electricity case. M2 §2.28, M3 §3.19, M6 §§6.32–6.33, M8 §8.31, and M9 §§9.25–9.26 examine reported tables, measurement, causal interpretation, public reassurance, ontology, and household authority. Four complete Python programs and five original TikZ figures support the comparisons. Guided practice is optional and ungraded.

The [Statistical Inference lab](Statistical_Inference/README.md) includes argument reconstruction, inverse power, and counterexamples to unsupported extrapolation and harmlessness claims. The practice remains optional and ungraded.

## Institutional Evidence

The optional [Statistical Inference lab](Statistical_Inference/README.md) examines price and bill denominators, project timelines, donor exposure, and cost-allocation rules through complete synthetic examples.

## Philosophical Arguments and Stakeholder Reasons

[Optional practice](Philosophical_Arguments/README.md) constructs an ought conclusion, defends its premises, compares affected positions, and answers an objection. The electricity case examines public reassurance, publication choices, and residents' opportunities to challenge evidence.

- [Qualitative Evidence](Qualitative_Evidence/README.md): interpret utility disclosures with explicit coding rules, negative cases, independent review, and stakeholder arguments. Optional ungraded practice.

## Pricing, Individual Reasons, and Antitrust Evidence

[Scanlon Pricing](Scanlon_Pricing/README.md) offers optional ungraded practice in demand identification, individual burdens, experimental risk, Kantian authority, and a hypothetical antitrust case. Five complete programs accompany the argument exercise.

## Analytical Arguments and Counterexamples

The [Philosophical Arguments practice](Philosophical_Arguments/README.md) includes a five-step argument record, two exact Python constructions, and pricing, electricity, and mHealth applications. Students test premise preservation, identify missing evidence or reasons, and state a condition for revising their conclusion. The exercise is public optional ungraded practice and supplies no course-wide permission to use AI.

## Explanation and Learning Evaluation

[AI-Augmented Learning](AI_Augmented_Learning/README.md) is optional ungraded practice in predicting a bill, preserving an argument across representations, calculating percentage changes, separating feature effects, and defending a revised conclusion. It runs without an AI account and adds no assessment rule.

## Conflicts of Interest and Consumer Control

[Optional ungraded practice](Conflicts_of_Interest/README.md) develops a duty-based conflict analysis, reproduces the synthetic feed comparison, and constructs a Kantian and Scanlonian argument with a feasible alternative. It also connects professional dissent to Confucian remonstrance.

## Bad Controls and Thoughtful Research Design

The [optional ungraded practice](Bad_Controls/README.md) asks students to defend controls and sample restrictions, reproduce the project-window comparisons, and evaluate whether the evidence justifies reassurance to households.

## Data, Truth, and Norms

[Optional ungraded practice](Data_Truth_and_Norms/README.md) connects selected evidence and causal counterfactuals to the artifact analogy, distinct norms, and an argument about institutional duties to provide household evidence.

## Evidence Standards and Reasonable Protection

[Optional ungraded practice](Evidence_Standards/README.md) compares exact synthetic error probabilities, opposing loss specifications, demand restrictions, and a philosophical argument for action under uncertainty. It connects measurement history to pricing and household electricity through an identified person, a supported concern, a normative premise, a feasible alternative, and conditions for revision.

It is optional and ungraded. Read M9 §§9.70–9.75 and the [15-source guide](../01_Resources/49_Online_Engagement_and_Agency/README.md).
