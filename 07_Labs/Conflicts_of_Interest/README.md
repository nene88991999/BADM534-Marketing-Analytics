# Engagement, Judgment, and Conflicts of Interest

Optional ungraded practice. The [worked example](../../03_Data_and_Code/Conflicts_of_Interest/README.md) uses constructed adult user-day totals. Read M9 §§9.45–9.49 and the [source guide](../../01_Resources/15_Marketing_Dialectics/09_Conflicts_of_Interest/README.md).

## Define the Decision and Responsibility

A firm asks its analyst to recommend a feed that increases advertising contribution. Some users have asked to stop after a specified duration. The analyst also prepares a public claim that the chosen design benefits consumers. Identify the responsibility governing that claim, the people to whom reasons are owed, and the incentive that could interfere with the judgment. Distinguish this conflict from the ordinary difference between the firm's and the user's preferred outcomes.

Write the commercial objective and the customer's purpose separately. Explain which outcomes the firm could omit while still producing an accurate estimate of engagement. Identify the evidence needed to detect that omission and the person with authority to require a response.

## Construct a Reasonable Principle

Write a proposed principle and a feasible alternative. Identify a factual premise, a normative premise, and an ought conclusion. Formulate objections from a time-limited user, a user who values extended browsing, and a worker or creator who depends on the service. Compare the burdens of both principles from those positions. Avoid treating votes or a sum of predicted clicks as the reasonable-rejection criterion (Scanlon, 1998).

Now vary one premise: the user deliberately revises the stopping instruction; the alternative imposes a substantial accessibility cost; or the apparent request results from a measurement error. Explain whether and why your recommendation changes. Preserve the distinction between challenging a premise and changing the principle solely to favor the firm.

## Assess the Analyst's Incentives

Suppose the analyst's bonus depends on launching the winning design. Propose a concrete change to compensation, review authority, data access, or publication rights. Explain which pressure it addresses and what would make it ineffective. Assess whether disclosure alone addresses the relevant duty. Connect truthful challenge within the firm to Confucian remonstrance and the customer's effective ability to contest the decision.

Apply the same reasoning to personalized pricing or electricity reassurance. Identify the missing bridge from the measured outcome to the broader claim. Criticize that inference without inventing a motive, funding relationship, or finding of misconduct. State evidence or reasons that would change your criticism.

## Marketing Publications and Unwanted Continuation

Read M9 §§9.70–9.75 and the [publication guide](../../01_Resources/49_Online_Engagement_and_Agency/README.md). This exercise is optional and ungraded. Use catalog IDs E01–E15 to keep each claim attached to its source.

### Classify the Evidence

For each statement, identify the supporting observation and the strongest conclusion it warrants.

1. A company report cites a study of logged streaks.
2. A company reports greater retention alongside fewer users reaching a daily goal.
3. A historical launch announcement describes rewards for extended viewing.
4. Repetition increases among people who entered an uncertain-reward task, while initial entry falls.
5. A study's value-added activity means client-survey participation in a market-research app.
6. A regulator announces preliminary concerns about addictive design.

Classify each as a mechanism finding, deployed practice, explicit research connection, reported outcome, or preliminary regulatory finding. More than one category may apply. Identify the additional evidence needed before claiming clinical addiction, a particular paper's causal influence on a product, or final legal liability.

### Reconstruct a Commercial Case

Choose the streak or binge-viewing case. Write the chronology, the company's stated purpose, the observed or reported outcome, and a consumer interest that the outcome leaves unresolved. Preserve the strongest supported benefit: repeated learning, reduced ad interruption, or free delayed access. Explain how that benefit affects the objection without assuming that it settles every user's circumstances.

Revise this claim into a defensible statement: “Marketing research proves that this company deliberately addicted its customers.” Identify exactly which unsupported steps you removed. Then state a serious ethical criticism whose factual premises the record can support. Criticism becomes more useful when its strongest conclusion survives a careful reading of the evidence.

### Evaluate a Feasible Alternative

Run the two existing feed-policy commands above and read the complete program in M9 §9.48. Preserve its synthetic population and separate group denominators. Compare the contribution-based recommendation with an alternative that honors a valid stopping instruction. Explain why these calculations cannot estimate the effects of the real products in the reading guide.

Construct a factual premise, a normative premise, and an ought conclusion. Compare the reasons of a time-limited user, a user who welcomes extended use, and a person whose income depends on the service. Specify a feasible alternative and the evidence needed to assess its burdens. Consider whether a reversible safeguard could be justified while a clinical question remains unresolved.

### Assess the Strongest Reply

Use the smartphone-reduction study as a beneficial application of habit research. Explain why its presence changes a blanket objection to behavioral design. Then identify a design condition under which your narrower criticism remains valid. Ask an AI assistant to challenge the argument, verify its source claims, and retain any reply that exposes an unsupported premise.
