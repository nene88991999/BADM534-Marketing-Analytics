# Replication, Evidence, and Responsibility

An insurer is considering a change to its reporting form. Its analyst cites a study that has since been retracted. Decide what the insurer ought to do with the recommendation and what evidence it should seek next. This is optional ungraded practice; released assignment documents determine any graded requirements.

## Examine the Artifact

Run [assess_records.py](../../03_Data_and_Code/Research_Integrity/assess_records.py) and read its [input documentation](../../03_Data_and_Code/Research_Integrity/README.md). State the policy unit, missing-car rule, unknown time intervals, font strata, and denominator before interpreting an output. Reconcile one policy's stored difference with the available car readings without publishing its masked identifier.

Explain why an unusually flat histogram is a lead for investigation. Identify two benign processes that could produce a sharp boundary or different rounding, then state what source evidence could distinguish them from fabrication. Examine the joint four-car pattern. Explain why the matching rule is informative and why it is not a probability that a named person committed fraud.

## Separate the Claims

Compare the [source records](../../01_Resources/52_Research_Integrity_and_Replication/README.md). Make a table with columns for the claim, source, evidence inspected, unresolved question, and warranted action. Include numerical reproduction, a failed replication, an expression of concern, a retraction, and an author's statement about an investigation. Preserve the distinctions among these statuses.

Write a defensible replacement for an insurer's recommendation based on the retracted study. Address the customer's reasons and the cost of substituting a persuasive behavioral claim for evidence about actual reporting. Do not infer that a failure to replicate proves fraud or that a retraction identifies the fabricator.

## Allocate Responsibility

Use [M9 §9.65.7](../../06_Lecture_Notes/modules/M09_Marketing_Dialectics.md) to distinguish causal contribution, duties of a role, accountability, culpability, and repair. Assign specific obligations to the source-data custodian, analyst, coauthors, journal, press office, and later user of the finding. State what each could know and control. Explain why uncertainty about personal blame need not postpone correction of an unreliable recommendation.

Compare three access arrangements: an open de-identified artifact, independently governed secure access, and sponsor-selected access with a veto over adverse publication. Identify which parts of the investigation each permits. Justify restrictions through their protected interests and feasible alternatives. Connect the result to the [Kilts policy case](../../01_Resources/45_Data_Truth_and_Norms/06_Corporate_Standing/KILTS_CASE.md), without claiming that those policies caused the integrity failures in the signing study.
