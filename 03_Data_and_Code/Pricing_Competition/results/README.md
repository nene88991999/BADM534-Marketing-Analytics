All rows average over the same equally likely demand states. Costs and preferences stay fixed. Monetary totals are dollars per market-day for two restaurants and their customers; quantities are total meal equivalents per market-day. Posted prices are state-weighted means in dollars per meal equivalent.

Within independent pricing, perfect information raises expected profit by $1.19 and reduces expected consumer surplus by $1.48. Mean posted price and expected quantity remain unchanged. Prices fall in the low state and rise in the high state, shifting consumption away from the state with higher marginal willingness to pay. The quantity-weighted paid price rises from $5.33 to $5.47. This identifies why a single mean-price measure can miss a welfare change under the stated model.

Holding the uninformative signal fixed, joint pricing raises expected profit by $3.70 and lowers consumer surplus by $12.96 relative to independent pricing. Total quantity falls by 2.22 meal equivalents. The two comparisons change different objects. Neither supplies empirical evidence of a pricing agreement or an actual restaurant overcharge.

The CSV includes the intermediate accuracy 0.75. The JSON preserves exact fractions, so rounded components in a printed row need not sum to the rounded total. The model's welfare includes the representative consumer and the two firms; income heterogeneity, nutrition, employment, externalities, and vendor costs are outside its specified payoffs.
