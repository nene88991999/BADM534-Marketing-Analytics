# Qualitative Content Analysis of Cost Recovery

## Question and Analytical Approach

Under what conditions do utilities describe infrastructure investment reaching household charges, and which revenues or contractual protections may offset those charges? Use directed qualitative content analysis, beginning with recovery, allocation, timing, and offsets. Retain a category for material that does not fit. Hsieh and Shannon (2005, pp. 1281–1283) identify both the value of theoretically informed categories and the risk of favoring confirmatory evidence. Full references and links appear in the [methods guide](../../01_Resources/42_Qualitative_Evidence/README.md).

The prior expectation is that infrastructure costs may reach households. Record what would change that expectation: disallowed recovery, direct assignment to large loads, enforceable guarantees, or net bill reductions supported by an appropriate comparison. Assess these possibilities with the same care as passages describing higher charges. Commercial self-interest is a reason to inspect an assertion's basis; it does not establish that the assertion is false.

## Sampling and Context

This teaching selection follows known leads and includes documents with contrasting claims. It is neither a systematic review nor an independent test of a prespecified theory. Broader research should define utility jurisdictions, observation dates, document types, and inclusion rules before retrieval; include disconfirming cases; retain unavailable documents; and record how later sampling responds to unresolved explanations. Six documents do not establish thematic saturation.

Use a claim within a complete speaker turn or document paragraph as the coding unit. Read the surrounding discussion and the question that prompted an answer. Record the issuer, operating subsidiary, customer class, time horizon, units, speaker role, and prepared-remarks or Q&A context. A parent-company investor call does not identify every subsidiary's rate treatment. A syndicated transcript and its issuer PDF are renditions of the same event. The later Evergy release adds documentary specificity within the same corporate source family.

## Coding Rules

| Field | Inclusion Rule | Important Exclusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `forecast` | A speaker expects a future outcome | A forecast does not establish realization |
| `request` | Recovery or rates are explicitly sought | Filing does not establish approval |
| `proposal` | An announced settlement or arrangement awaits the relevant disposition | Expected savings do not establish household receipts |
| `accounting_mechanism` | An accounting policy describes how costs enter recoverable amounts | Capitalization alone does not identify a residential allocation |
| `approved_price_illustration` | A regulator's announced auction result is translated into a stated-use bill | A bill illustration is distinct from observed household billing data |
| `regulator_explanation` | An agency identifies drivers or a pass-through arrangement | An explanation supplies no causal share without an identified comparison |

Code `direction` separately: increase, offset, mechanism, mixed, or unclear. Code the comparison, including current bills, a no-growth scenario, inflation, or another tariff. A claimed offset relative to higher counterfactual costs can coexist with a nominal bill increase. Retain both claims. Leave unidentified shares unspecified. The public codebook does not contain a label called “proof of harm.”

## Contextual Reading and Comparison

Read the entire selected document before relying on retrieved windows. Keyword searches prioritize passages; they can miss synonyms or match denials and analyst questions. Inspect a sample of unmatched passages and preserve reasons for exclusion. Record changes to definitions and revisit earlier coding after a revision. Complete source turns remain in the local cache; the public matrix supplies locators and original paraphrases.

Compare claims within an issuer before comparing issuers. Organize the matrix by mechanism, population, timing, evidence stage, and alternative explanation. This adapts the charting and within-case comparison described by Gale et al. (2013, stages 2–7). Similar words about “recovery” can concern gas infrastructure, electric supply procurement, or distribution construction. Preserve those differences before developing an explanatory theme.

For triangulation, identify what each source can establish. A call indicates an attributed position; a filing documents a request; an order establishes a regulatory disposition; tariffs and billing data support different outcome claims. Agreement between a company call and its own release does not supply independent corroboration. A regulator's illustration strengthens an institutional interpretation without identifying the contribution of a specific data center.

## Independent Review and Reflexivity

For a shared codebook exercise, two readers independently annotate the same complete passages before discussion. Keep their original records, compare disagreements, and document why a definition or interpretation changed. Use `data/reviewer_template.csv` for separate submissions. O'Connor and Joffe (2020, practical considerations) discuss when this procedure can clarify a coding frame. No independent human coding, agreement statistic, or accuracy estimate has been completed for this release.

The procedure fits a structured codebook analysis. Braun and Clarke (2021) explain why coding reliability is not a universal requirement for reflexive thematic analysis. An LLM can suggest passages or competing readings, with its prompt and model recorded. Repeated outputs from one model do not constitute independent human confirmation. Human agreement also cannot establish the truth of the underlying corporate claim.

Keep a reflexive memo stating the initial view, source-selection decisions, difficult interpretations, and revisions. SRQR (O'Brien et al., 2014, Table 1) supports transparent reporting of these choices and the connection between findings and empirical material. A quality checklist cannot replace substantive judgment.

## Stakeholders and Transferability

Investor calls reveal how management justifies investment and earnings expectations to capital providers. They provide limited access to household experience, ability to contest charges, or affordability tradeoffs. Extending the study to residents would require a separate sampling and consent process, alongside public comments, consumer-counsel filings, and billing evidence where appropriate. Do not infer residents' consent or silence from their absence in a corporate transcript.

The defensible result identifies documented mechanisms, competing claims, and the conditions under which an assurance needs further support. Generalization depends on institutional similarity and additional evidence. Counting selected adverse passages supplies no estimate of how often utilities transfer data-center costs to households.
