## Course Description

Marketing decisions require evidence about customer preferences, behavior, and responses to a firm's actions. The course applies measurement, visualization, regression, conjoint analysis, segmentation, lifetime value, text and network analysis, experiments, causal inference, prediction, and recommendation to product design, acquisition, retention, pricing, and budgets.

Students use AI-assisted Python to specify questions, verify calculations, and interpret results. Marketing Dialectics applies Kantian, Confucian, and Scanlonian philosophy to agency, conflicts of interest, and regulation. Disciplinary history and the phenomena–noumena distinction (Kant, 1781/1787) connect measurement to persons and obligations. Chicago price theory supplies cases on willingness to pay, rational addiction, compensation, and market justification. Students assess objectives, evidentiary standards, false alarms, and missed harm, applying the same standards to claims and criticisms.

## Learning Objectives

- Define a managerial decision, the relevant population, and the evidence needed to inform it.
- Evaluate which ends deserve pursuit, who has authority to select them, and what obligations the decision creates, distinguishing empirical knowledge from practical justification.
- Assess how data are produced and accessed; define observation units, denominators, missing values, and reproducible transformations.
- Apply marketing analytics methods; distinguish descriptive patterns, prediction, and causal effects; justify controls, populations, and observation windows.
- Evaluate AI-assisted workflows through independent checks, statistical design, meaningful-effect bounds, and the costs of false positives and missed harm.
- Justify recommendations using evidence, clear figures, customer consequences, and critical examination of ethical premises.
- Reconstruct arguments; distinguish fit, identification, and valuation; justify investigation or protection under uncertainty; state reasons for revising a conclusion.

## Class Format and Materials

Meetings combine instructor-led explanations, worked examples, Python, and discussion. Students predict results, inspect equations and code, and defend or revise conclusions. The 15 meetings last 170 minutes each and include two project discussion and presentation sessions; presentation requirements will be specified separately.

The [course repository](https://github.com/nene88991999/BADM534-Marketing-Analytics) provides [lecture notes](../06_Lecture_Notes/README.md), [session slides](../05_Slides/README.md), and [data with runnable Python](../03_Data_and_Code/README.md). Markdown companions support search and LLM use. Resource recommendations provide background; assigned readings will be identified separately.
